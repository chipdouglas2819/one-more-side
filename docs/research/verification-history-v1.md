# v1 verification history: reviews, economy analyses, playtests, fixes


## Browser playtest

**fun notes:** The core hook works: tapping the blob-die immediately gives a number pop and a currency tick, and the "one more side" conceit pays off visually — the die is literally a circle at 1 side and visibly gains a flat edge, then becomes a lens, triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon as you buy sides. That shape-morph is the single best thing about the prototype and is worth protecting/polishing. Buying the auto-roller and watching the counter climb on its own without tapping felt like a satisfying payoff moment.

**pacing notes:** First purchase (+SIDE for 10) landed in under a minute of real taps — good early hook. Growing from 2 sides to 5 sides took a few minutes of steady tapping (costs 22 -> 49 -> 107), which felt reasonable. Getting from 5 sides to the 6-sides/auto-roller unlock (235 then 600) is a much bigger grind — at pre-roller tap rates (roughly 1-4 currency per successful tap, one tap per ~1s) this is many minutes of uninterrupted manual tapping with no assist, which risks feeling like a slog right before the payoff that's supposed to relieve the tapping. I sped through the last stretch with the ?dev=1 currency values to verify the 6-side/roller/late-game state within a reasonable review window.

**mobile layout notes:** No overflow or clipping observed on the main screen, the shop cards, or the Settings modal at 375x812. The only layout issue seen was in the ?dev=1 debug toolbar, whose buttons (+1K/+1M/+10 MIN/OFFLINE 2H/AD FAIL/WIPE) visually overlap the "ROLLER — AT 6 SIDES" shop card text underneath it; that's a dev-only overlay so low real-world impact.

**persistence notes:** Reload correctly preserved side count, roller level, and currency (localStorage key oms.save.v1), and even applied idle/offline earnings on top after a short gap. I did not see the "WELCOME BACK" panel trigger in my session (it exists in the DOM but stayed hidden) — the away-gaps I created via reload may have been too short to cross its threshold, so I could not verify its collect/double-via-ad flow end-to-end.

**Best moments:**
- The die's shape literally morphing from a circle to a triangle to a square to a pentagon to a hexagon as you buy sides is a genuinely charming, on-theme payoff for the 'ONE MORE SIDE' name.
- Buying the first upgrade (10 currency) happened within about a minute of taps with zero instructions needed.
- Turning on the auto-roller and watching the currency climb hands-free was a satisfying idle-loop confirmation moment.
- No console errors surfaced at any point, including through sound toggling and settings/modal interactions.

**Findings:**
- [major] **Die face numbers are wrong / skip 5 and duplicate other values** (Die face-label / MAX ROLL rendering logic (client-side, index.html)): On the 5-sided die the five visible faces read 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 (never a 5). After buying the 6th side the six faces read 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9 (still no 5, and 6/9 don't match a normal d6). The header 'MAX ROLL' stat is also inconsistent with the actual side count: it showed 6 while the die still only had 5 sides, and 9 once the die had 6 sides. This is reproducible every time a new side tier is purchased. Suggested fix: Face values for an N-sided die should be a straightforward 1..N sequence, and MAX ROLL should equal the current side count (or the actual highest generated value on that die), not a separately-computed number. Check whatever formula picks the label for the last face / the MAX ROLL stat — it looks like it's reading a different variable (maybe a roller multiplier or an unrelated 'max theoretical roll') instead of the die's own side count.
- [major] **Rapid multi-tapping silently drops most taps** (Roll input handling / animation lock (likely a cooldown gated on the roll animation duration)): A burst of 10 clicks fired back-to-back with no delay only registered about 2 successful rolls (verified twice). Taps spaced roughly 1 second apart register reliably. There's no error and no visible 'tap ignored' feedback beyond a faint ghost-trail on the die, so a player mashing the die (very natural in an idle/clicker game) will feel like most of their inputs are being eaten. Suggested fix: Either queue taps that arrive during the roll animation (resolve them immediately after, even if visually batched), or shorten the roll-lock window so it doesn't eat clicks during normal fast tapping. At minimum, add a light visual/haptic cue when a tap is ignored so it doesn't feel broken.
- [minor] **?dev=1 debug buttons (+1K etc.) don't do anything** (Dev toolbar buttons under ?dev=1): Clicking the '+1K' dev button (and I'd expect the sibling +1M/+10 MIN/OFFLINE 2H buttons) produced no change to the displayed currency or to the oms.save.v1 localStorage record (coins and savedAt stayed identical before/after the click), even though the click landed on the correct button element. Suggested fix: Not player-facing, but worth fixing since it's the tool meant to let you QA late-game balance quickly — wire the +1K/+1M/etc. handlers to actually mutate and persist the save state.
- [minor] **Dev toolbar overlaps the ROLLER shop card at phone width** (Dev toolbar layout under ?dev=1, 375px viewport): With ?dev=1 open at 375px width, the debug button row (+1K/+1M/+10 MIN/OFFLINE 2H/AD FAIL/WIPE) sits directly on top of the 'ROLLER — AT 6 SIDES' shop card, obscuring its subtitle text. Suggested fix: Dev-only cosmetic issue; give the debug bar its own row/margin above the shop list so it doesn't overlap, or make it collapsible.

## Code review

**Overall:** Read SPEC.md in full and all 1933 lines of index.html. The economy core is genuinely good: `expectedPayout` is a correct closed-form inclusion-exclusion over the landed-index array (I verified the pair-minus-triple identity and the `setMatchEV` probability weighting), every displayed price comes from ECON, purchases re-check funds in both `canBuy` and `buy` so there is no insufficient-funds or negative-cost exploit, the 100ms tap cooldown holds under multi-touch, offline earnings are clamped to `[0, cap]` so clock manipulation cannot exceed the 4h/8h cap, and the roll-resolves-then-renderer-plays-back architecture is respected throughout. The `recastThreshold` scaling by M is a smart fix to a real hoarding exploit the spec missed.

Sixteen defects found. Two are blockers: (1) `landResult` is gated on the settle tween completing, but the auto-roll interval is shorter than the 380ms animation at roller levels 1 through 4, so the game loses all floating numbers, land sounds and edge flashes across the entire mid-game — and the same gate silences feedback for anyone tapping faster than ~2.6/s in the first two minutes; (2) `Store.get` never reads Capacitor Preferences, making it a write-only mirror and leaving the save only as durable as WebView localStorage on the Android target, the exact inversion of what the spec specifies.

Five majors: the Hot Hand cooldown lives in `performance.now()` time and is wiped on reload, giving an unlimited ×3 via refresh; a pending OFFLINE DOUBLE resumed after a crash grants exactly zero because `applyOfflineDouble` ignores its payload and reads a stale in-memory value; the DOUBLE button is permanently disabled after one use per page load; `fitScale` and `unitX` disagree on spacing so two dice overlap by ~27px and three by ~8px at 360px; and the S=2 lens outline winds opposite to every other shape, so the 1→2 and 2→3 morphs — the transition the spec's build order says to validate before writing anything else — interpolate through a self-intersecting shear rather than a squash-and-split.

I would fix the two blockers and the morph winding before putting this on a phone again, since the morph is the thing the spec says to judge the whole idea on and it currently cannot be judged fairly.


**Findings:**
- [blocker] **No floating payout numbers, land sound, or edge flash at roller levels 1–4 (most of the mid-game)** (index.html:1590-1596 (auto-roll resets an.t), index.html:1602-1611 (settle + landResult gate), index.html:1532-1533 (onTap)): `landResult()` — which spawns the floating `+N`, plays the land blip, and sets the white edge flash — is only reached from `update()` at line 1608: `if (a.t >= 1 && i === 0 && G.lastResult) landResult(G.lastResult);`. It requires the settle animation to actually complete (`a.t` reaching 1 over `a.dur = 0.38`). But the auto-roll block at lines 1590-1593 unconditionally resets `an.t = 0` on every new roll. With `autoRate(L) = 2.0 * 1.35^L`, the inter-roll interval is 0.500s at L0, then 0.370s (L1), 0.274s (L2), 0.203s (L3), 0.151s (L4) — all shorter than the 0.380s animation. So from roller level 1 through level 4 the die never finishes settling, `landResult` is never called, and the player sees a permanently spinning shape with zero payout feedback, no sound, and no notch flash. It only comes back at L5 (8.97/s), where `G.fastMode` kicks in and `update()` calls `spawnFloat`/`Audio2.play('land')` directly at line 1596. The same dead zone hits manual play: `onTap` (line 1532) also resets `an.t = 0`, so any player tapping faster than ~2.6 taps/s in the first two minutes gets no `+1` floats at all. This is the entire feedback loop of the game going silent for the bulk of a session. Suggested fix: Decouple payout feedback from animation completion. Call `landResult(res)` at the moment the roll is resolved (in `doRoll` and in the auto-roll block) rather than when the tween finishes, and let the settle tween only drive rotation/squash. If you want the number to appear on landing rather than on launch, shorten `an.dur` to `Math.min(0.38, 0.8 / throwRate)` so the animation always fits inside the roll interval, and keep the `a.t >= 1` hook only for the visual flash.
- [blocker] **Capacitor Preferences is written but never read — the save is only ever as durable as WebView localStorage** (index.html:726-750 (Store), index.html:760-773 (load)): `Store.get` (lines 735-738) reads `localStorage` and then the in-memory fallback, and never touches `cap`. `cap` is only used in `set` (line 742) and `remove` (line 747). So Capacitor Preferences is a write-only mirror that no code path ever reads back. SPEC.md §5 specifies the opposite: "Capacitor Preferences as the store with a localStorage mirror." On the stated Android target this matters: WebView localStorage is cleared by 'Clear cache/storage', by some OEM storage managers, and is not guaranteed to survive app updates, while Preferences (SharedPreferences) is. The moment localStorage is cleared, `load()` returns 0, `newState()` is used, and the player's entire run is gone even though the durable copy exists on disk. Suggested fix: Make boot async: if `Capacitor.Plugins.Preferences` exists, `await Preferences.get({key})` first and fall back to `localStorage` only when Preferences returns null (and immediately write the localStorage value back into Preferences to migrate existing players). `boot()` already runs after DOMContentLoaded, so making it `async` and awaiting a `Store.getAsync()` before `load()` is a contained change.
- [major] **Hot Hand cooldown and buff are wiped on reload, giving an unlimited ×3 multiplier** (index.html:687 (clock), index.html:768-769 (load resets), index.html:1329-1343 (hotState/startHot)): `clock()` is `performance.now() / 1000` (line 687), which restarts near zero on every page load. `startHot` (lines 1338-1340) stores `st.hotUntil` and `st.hotReadyAt` in that page-local timebase, and `load()` explicitly zeroes both (lines 768-769, "buff clocks are page-local, never restored"). `hotState()` (lines 1329-1336) then returns 'ready' immediately after any reload because `clock() < st.hotReadyAt` is false. The loop is: watch the rewarded ad, get ×3 for 120s, reload, watch again — the 20-minute `HOT_COOLDOWN_S` is unenforceable. `multiplier()` applies HOT_MULT to every roll (lines 474-478), so this is a permanent ×3 on all income for the cost of a page refresh, which breaks the pacing the whole `SIDE_GROWTH` retune was done to protect. Suggested fix: Store `hotUntil`/`hotReadyAt` as absolute wall-clock milliseconds (`Date.now()`), restore them in `load()` instead of zeroing, and compare against `Date.now()` in `hotState`/`multiplier`. Clamp a restored `hotUntil` to at most `Date.now() + HOT_DURATION_S * 1000` so a forward clock jump can't extend a live buff, and treat a backwards clock jump (`hotReadyAt - Date.now() > HOT_COOLDOWN_S * 1000`) as cooldown-complete.
- [major] **A pending OFFLINE DOUBLE reward resumed after a crash grants exactly zero coins** (index.html:1379-1390 (applyOfflineDouble/onOfflineDouble), index.html:851-874 (grantReward/resumePendingReward), index.html:1874-1875 (boot order)): `runRewarded('OFFLINE DOUBLE', { seconds: seconds }, applyOfflineDouble)` (line 1389) correctly writes the pending record with the away time in the payload. `grantReward` calls `fn(payload)` (line 855). But `applyOfflineDouble` (lines 1379-1384) takes no parameter and reads `G.offlineSeconds` from memory instead. On a resume-with-pending-record — the exact case SPEC.md §6 calls non-negotiable — `resumePendingReward()` runs at line 1874, before `handleReturn(away)` at line 1875, at which point `G.offlineSeconds` is still its initial 0. So `E.offlineEarnings(st, 0, true)` returns 0, `G.offlinePending` is set to 0, and `collectOffline()` credits nothing and closes a panel that was never opened. The player watched an ad and got nothing; then `handleReturn` opens the normal card with the un-doubled amount. `st.pending` is cleared either way, so it cannot be recovered. Suggested fix: Change the signature to `function applyOfflineDouble(payload) { var secs = (payload && payload.seconds) || G.offlineSeconds; var full = E.offlineEarnings(st, secs, true); ... }`. Also move `resumePendingReward()` to after `handleReturn(away)` so a resumed double lands on the card the player is actually looking at, and have the resumed path credit coins directly rather than going through `collectOffline()`'s panel-close.
- [major] **WATCH AD · DOUBLE IT is permanently disabled after the first use in a session** (index.html:1386-1390 (onOfflineDouble), index.html:1352-1364 (handleReturn)): `onOfflineDouble` sets `$('#offDouble').disabled = true;` (line 1388) and nothing ever re-enables it. `handleReturn` (lines 1352-1364) re-opens the offline card on every resume >60s and sets `#offDouble`'s `style.display`, but never touches `.disabled`. Because the same page instance stays alive across many background/foreground cycles on Android (the `visibilitychange` handler at lines 1913-1922 calls `handleReturn` again), the player can double their offline earnings exactly once per cold start and the button is dead-but-visible for every subsequent welcome-back card. That is both a player-facing bug and a direct loss of the primary rewarded-ad placement's impressions. Suggested fix: Add `$('#offDouble').disabled = false;` inside `handleReturn` next to the `style.display` line, and also re-enable it in `collectOffline()` before `closePanel`.
- [major] **Two and three dice visually overlap at 360px — fitScale and unitX use different spacing assumptions** (index.html:1036-1048 (fitScale, unitX)): `fitScale` (lines 1036-1042) budgets `2.3 * maxR` of horizontal width per die across `CW * 0.94`, but `unitX` (lines 1044-1048) places centres at `CW / (count + 1) * (i + 1)`, which is a tighter spacing. Worked through at the spec's 360x740 target (hud 56 + readout 64 + shop ~298 leaves CH≈322, groundY≈270, so the width term binds): with 2 dice at 20 sides, `maxR = 140 * 0.72 = 100.8`, `wNeed = 463.7`, `scale = 338.4 / 463.7 = 0.730`, giving `R = 73.6` and a die width of 147px — while `unitX` puts the centres only 120px apart. The two dice overlap by roughly 27px. With 3 dice: `maxR = 81.2`, `scale = 0.604`, `R = 49.1`, width 98px against 90px centre spacing — an 8px overlap. The overlap is scale-invariant, so it happens at every side count once the width constraint binds, i.e. on every phone at 360px. Suggested fix: Make the two agree. Either change `unitX` to `CW * 0.03 + (i + 0.5) * (CW * 0.94 / count)` so centres are spaced by exactly the per-die budget `fitScale` reserved, or change `wNeed` to `(count + 1) * maxR * 2.3 * count / (count + 1)`. The first is cleaner: one slot per die of width `CW*0.94/count`, die centred in its slot.
- [major] **The S=2 lens outline winds in the opposite direction from every other shape, so the 1→2 and 2→3 morphs fold through themselves** (index.html:960-992 (outline: S=1 at 966-971, S=2 at 976-985, S>=3 at 987-991), index.html:1273-1290 (startMorph)): `startMorph` (lines 1273-1290) lerps 240 arc-length-resampled points point-for-point between `outline(oldSides)` and `outline(newSides)`, which only reads correctly if both outlines start at the same place and traverse in the same direction. They do for S=1 and S>=3: `outline` for S>=3 (lines 987-991) starts at the ground contact `[0, contactDepth]` and walks vertices at increasing angle (v[0] is at `BOTTOM + PI/S`, i.e. bottom-left); `outline(1)` (lines 966-971) starts at `[0, R*cut]` (the flat's midpoint) and also walks at increasing angle from `BOTTOM + half` (first point `(-0.6R, 0.8R)`, bottom-left). But `outline(2)` (lines 976-985) starts at the LEFT TIP `(-R, 0)` and its lower arc runs left-tip → bottom → right-tip, i.e. angle decreasing (pi → pi/2 → 0). So the 1→2 morph maps the bottom of the circle onto the left tip of the lens and reverses the winding, and the 2→3 morph does the same in reverse. The intermediate frames are a self-intersecting shear, not a squash-and-split. SPEC.md §Build order names exactly this transition as the thing to validate before writing anything else, and §5 calls it "the money shot" — it is the first thing a new player sees at 0:10. Suggested fix: Rewrite the S=2 branch to match the convention: start at the ground contact `[0, h]`, then walk the lower arc toward the LEFT tip first (angle increasing), around the upper arc, and back along the lower arc to the contact point — i.e. emit the same point order the polygon branch does. Cheapest correct fix is to build the current array and then rotate/reverse it: find the index nearest `[0, h]`, `reverse()` the array, and rotate it so that index is element 0. Verify by rendering the 1→2 and 2→3 morphs frame-stepped before shipping.
- [minor] **MAX ROLL readout goes stale after buying a second or third die** (index.html:1263-1267 (tryBuy unit branch), index.html:1619-1624 (maxRoll tick)): `tryBuy` sets `G.maxRollTarget = E.maxRoll(st.units)` when `kind === 'unit'` (line 1266) but never sets `G.maxRollTick`. `update()` only moves `G.maxRollShown` toward the target inside `if (G.maxRollTick > 0)` (lines 1619-1624), so the displayed number never changes. Buying die 2 raises the true max from `V(S1)` to `V(S1)+1` (the new die has 1 side, below `COMBO_MIN_SIDES`, so no combo), and buying die 3 adds another 1. The readout under the die therefore disagrees with `E.maxRoll` — the one number the spec describes as a promise to the player — until the next side purchase happens to correct it. Suggested fix: In the `kind === 'unit'` branch, mirror the side branch: capture `before` (already computed at line 1251), and after updating `G.maxRollTarget` set `if (G.maxRollTarget !== before) G.maxRollTick = 0.7;`.
- [minor] **load() does no type or range validation, so a corrupt or hand-edited save can soft-lock the game** (index.html:760-773 (load)): `load()` (lines 760-773) checks only `data.v === SAVE_VERSION` and `Array.isArray(data.units)`, then copies every matching key straight across: `for (var k in fresh) if (hasOwnProperty(data, k)) fresh[k] = data[k];`. `units: []` passes `Array.isArray` and produces a game with no dice at all — `sideTarget` returns null, `roll` credits 0, `fitScale` divides by zero, and there is no way out except a hard reset. `coins: "abc"`, `units: ["x"]`, `roller: 99`, or `slots: 500` all pass equally. localStorage is player-editable in a browser and in a debuggable WebView, and a partially-flushed write can also produce this. Once `st.coins` becomes NaN, `gte(NaN, cost)` is false forever so no purchase can be made, and `fmt(NaN)` renders as the infinity glyph. Suggested fix: Add a sanitise pass after the copy: coerce each numeric field with `var n = +data[k]; fresh[k] = isFinite(n) ? n : fresh[k];`; clamp `roller` to `[-1, MAX_ROLLER_LEVEL]`, `slots` to `[1, MAX_DICE]`, `shards`/`recasts`/`throws` to `>= 0`; and rebuild `units` as `data.units.slice(0, MAX_DICE).map(function(s){ s = Math.floor(+s); return isFinite(s) ? Math.min(MAX_SIDES, Math.max(1, s)) : 1; })`, falling back to `[1]` if the result is empty, then force `slots = Math.max(slots, units.length)`.
- [minor] **The 5-second debounced save never fires before the roller is bought** (index.html:1544-1563 (busy, frame), index.html:752-758 (save/markDirty)): `frame()` only accumulates `G.saveTimer` while the rAF loop is running (lines 1559-1563), and `busy()` (lines 1544-1551) returns false once the roll animation, coin lerp and floats have all finished — which, before `st.roller >= 0`, is about half a second after the last tap. So `G.saveTimer` is reset to 0 by the next `wake()` long before it reaches 5. In the pre-roller game the only saves are purchases, `visibilitychange` and `pagehide`. That covers ordinary app-switching, but a tab the OS discards without firing those (or a browser crash) loses the whole early run, and SPEC.md §5 specifies a 5s debounce that in practice never runs. Suggested fix: Move the debounce off the rAF loop: in `markDirty()`, if no save is scheduled, `G.saveHandle = setTimeout(function(){ G.saveHandle = 0; if (G.dirty) save(); }, 5000);` and clear the handle inside `save()`. Leave the existing `visibilitychange`/`pagehide` saves as they are.
- [minor] **Combo contribution exceeds the spec's 15% invariant whenever two dice have equal side counts** (index.html:280-283 (COMBO_MIN_SIDES rationale), index.html:374-415 (participants/comboFor/expectedPayout), SPEC.md:104 (invariant 4)): SPEC.md §4 invariant 4 requires combo contribution to stay at or below 15% of total measured income. With the implemented pair/triple combo (lines 376-415), for two participating dice with equal side counts S the closed form is `share = 2 / (S + 2)`: 33% at 4/4, 25% at 6/6, 16.7% at 10/10, 9.1% at 20/20. Equal counts are not a corner case — `sideTarget` (lines 483-490) auto-targets the fewest sides, so die 2 climbs to exactly match die 1 and the two sit equal (or one apart) for most of the two-dice phase. With three participating dice it is far worse: at [4,4,4] `expectedPayout` gives 12.66 against a base of 7.5, a 41% combo share. This is a real economy deviation, not just a doc mismatch — combo income is high-variance, so a larger share means the felt income swings more than the spec intends. Suggested fix: Either raise `COMBO_MIN_SIDES` (at 8, equal-count share caps at 20%; at 12 it caps at 14.3%) or drop `COMBO_PAIR_MULT` to a smaller bonus such as 1.5 for the three-die case. Whichever you pick, add an assertion to sim.js that walks every reachable `units` combination and fails if `E.comboShare(counts) > 0.15`, so this can't drift again.

- [minor] **Auto-roll income silently stops during the 700ms morph freeze and for as long as any panel is open** (index.html:1575-1599 (update auto-roll guard), index.html:1188 (frozen), index.html:1512-1517 (openPanel/closePanel)): The auto-roll accumulator only advances when `st.roller >= 0 && !frozen() && !G.panelOpen` (line 1575), and `G.autoAcc` is not banked while that guard is false — the elapsed time is simply discarded. - [minor] **Combo thread links die contact points instead of matching faces, and the gold landed-edge is applied to non-participating dice** (index.html:1779-1792 (combo thread), index.html:1730 and 1137 (opts.combo), index.html:1232-1242 (spawnFloat)): SPEC.md §3.6 describes the combo as readable from the visual: "matching faces glow and link with a light thread". Separately, `opts.combo` is `G.lastResult.combo > 1` (line 1730), a single global flag passed to every die, so `drawDie` colours the landed edge gold (line 1137) on dice that had nothing to do with the combo, including dice below `COMBO_MIN_SIDES` that `participants()` excluded. Suggested fix: Pass the matched set from `E.comboFor` (return the participating index list alongside the multiplier, or recompute `participants(st.units)` in draw) and use it three ways: draw the thread only between matched dice at the landed-edge midpoint `gy - contactDepth + 0` rather than the centre, set `opts.combo` per die from membership in that set, and anchor the float at `unitX(matchedSet[0], count)` when a combo fires.

## Economy analysis

**Overall:** The economy is well-built and the built-in simulator is unusually honest — it computes several of the numbers that indict it. What it does not do is check anything on a policy other than the reference bot, and that is where the two blockers live.

The serious one is a genuine deadlock. Because + SIDE auto-targets the smallest die and the combo multiplies the whole throw, there are 102 three-dice shapes where the only button the game will let you press lowers your income. My rush-die policy walked into 10/4/4 with the roller maxed and every slot owned, and from there bought nothing for 319 consecutive minutes; it never reached a Recast or end of content. sim.js already prints "102 + SIDE purchases lose EV, worst 3.88%" and passes it, because its bots quietly decline the losing purchase — but a real player has no other button. The per-purchase 3.88% also understates it: from 20/4/4 income sits below its starting level for 18 consecutive presses, 8.89% down at the trough.

The second blocker is the player who won't press Recast. Idle players hoard; this one hits a 52.3-minute wait with the shop greyed out (spec invariant 2 caps it at 45), and from 361 min the shop is permanently empty. sim.js hard-codes recasting into its run loop, so this player does not exist in the test suite.

On pacing: content ends at 99.9 min against a 150 min target, and 88.1 min for an active tapper — the latter is outside the ±35% band, but sim.js converts early misses on non-gate policies into PASS, so it never surfaces. SIDE_GROWTH 2.2 -> 2.3 plus UNLOCK_UNIT_SIDES 10 -> 9 puts every milestone in band with end-of-content at 144.7 min. The catch, worth deciding deliberately: that same change pushes the worst gap between side purchases from 490s to 1091s, and nothing I swept satisfies both the 150-minute target and the 600s ceiling. One of those two numbers in SPEC.md has to move.

Clean results, for what it is worth: no precision or overflow exposure anywhere (peak lifetime 3.3e9 against a 9.01e15 limit), prestige pays back in 9-11% of the climb, and there is no dominant strategy — + SIDE and ROLLER trade places as best-ROI roughly every other minute all game, which is the hardest thing on the list to get right and it is right.
