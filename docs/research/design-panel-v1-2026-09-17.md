# v1 design panel archive (2026-09-17)

Three independent designers each proposed a full design from a different angle (purist, face-builder, retention-and-ads). Three judges scored all three under different lenses. The v1 spec started from design 0 and grafted the judges' picks. Rejected ideas are kept here on purpose: several may be worth revisiting in a full build.


## Design 0: FACET — Build Your Die, Side by Side
**Hook:** You start with a one-sided die; every side you buy is a real object you can upgrade, re-carve into a Double, Reroll, Wild or Chain face, and watch physically re-form the die in front of you.

**Core loop:** Tap the die → it spins and lands on one of its owned faces → that face pays its value in Pips → spend Pips on the one thing the die visibly lacks. Three purchase verbs, always on screen, always comparable: BUY SIDE (polygon gains an edge, max roll goes up), UPGRADE FACE (one specific edge's number grows ×1.75), CARVE FACE (turn one edge into an effect face: ×2, Reroll, Wild, Chain). Later the loop widens without changing shape: an auto-roller rolls for you, a second die joins and the two dice are scored as a hand, and Recast melts everything back to a d1 while keeping one face you choose plus a permanent multiplier. Every decision is "which edge of this polygon do I improve", never "which of nine menus do I open".

**First five minutes:** 0:00 — One white disc on a dark screen with a single pip and the word TAP. No menu, no tutorial text. Tapping spins it; it always lands on 1; "+1" floats up. 0:12 — 25 Pips reached; the single button at the bottom, BUY SIDE — 25, has been pulsing since Pip 15. Pressing it triggers the signature moment: the disc splits into a two-edged lens, a "2" is engraved onto the new edge, screen shake, and the very next roll can show a 2. That single transition is the whole store listing. 0:35 — side 3 (triangle), 1:10 — side 4 (square). Around here a second button quietly appears: UPGRADE FACE — tapping any edge of the polygon selects it, and upgrading edge 1 from "1" to "1.75" teaches that faces are individual objects. 2:00 — side 5 (pentagon). 4:00 — side 6, and the die is a hexagon. The moment the 6th side lands, the game says one line: "A normal die. Keep going." That is the mission statement and the only copy in the first five minutes. Around 4:30 the CARVE button unlocks with exactly one option, DOUBLE (×2), priced so it lands about a minute later — the first time a face does something instead of just paying.

**Systems:**
- **The Die (faces as data):** Die = { faces: [Face], mult, autoLevel }. Face = { slot:int 1..n, type:'num'|'x2'|'reroll'|'wild'|'chain', level:int }. FaceValue = slot * 1.75^level. Everything in the game reads and writes this array; nothing else owns game state. This is the seam that makes the slot reskin a renderer swap.
- **Buy Side:** Appends a face { slot:n+1, type:'num', level:0 }. Raises the polygon's edge count by one and the expected roll by exactly +0.5 on a plain die. Always the headline purchase; always the most visible change.
- **Upgrade Face:** Per-face level. Tap an edge on the die itself to select it (no separate list UI until 12+ sides, when a compact ring list appears). Upgrading a high-slot face is far stronger than a low one, which creates a real 'which edge' decision by minute 10.
- **Carve Face (effects):** Converts one owned face's type, keeping its slot value and level. Five types, drip-fed one at a time: DOUBLE (pays its value and arms ×2 on the next roll, stacking), REROLL (pays and immediately rolls again, chainable), WILD (pays whatever the die's highest face is worth), CHAIN (pays, and pays more when other dice also land on Chain). Counts are capped as a fraction of n so the math stays bounded and builds stay legible.
- **Auto-Roller:** Unlocks once, never turns off, never asks for taps again. Rolls per second grows exponentially with level. Above 12 rolls/s the die stops animating individual rolls and becomes a blurred spinner with a running counter — a deliberate fix for the battery complaint that dogs the category leader.
- **Momentum (tapping stays relevant):** Each manual tap adds +15% payout for 3 seconds, stacking to +45%, decaying smoothly. Auto-roll never loses value; tapping is a voluntary burst. Nothing is ever gated behind tapping.
- **Second Die & Hands:** A second die starts at d1 and grows fast because the global multiplier has caught up. One tap rolls all dice together; matching slot numbers across dice multiply the whole tap. This is where CHAIN faces and the polygon-matching visuals pay off, and it is the exact evaluator the slot mode reuses as a payline.
- **Recast (prestige):** Melt everything back to a single d1, gain Shards for a permanent linear global multiplier, and keep ONE face of your choosing — its type and level survive into the new die. You get to watch the die grow again, faster, with a Wild or ×2 already sitting on it. Prestige rebuilds the toy instead of deleting it.
- **Shard Shop:** Five permanent, boring-on-purpose upgrades bought with Shards (momentum, head start at d3, longer offline cap, keep a second face on Recast, auto-roller head start). No second soft currency, no cards, no duels.

**Economy, currency flow:** ONE soft currency, Pips, earned only by rolling and spent only on the die. One prestige currency, Shards, earned only by Recasting and spent only in the Shard Shop. Neither is ever purchasable with real money, convertible, giftable or redeemable — this keeps the app entirely outside Play's Real-Money Gambling policy, and every purchase is a fixed disclosed price with no randomized crates, so loot-box odds disclosure never applies.

PER-ROLL INCOME (one die). Let n = sides. Face i has FaceValue(i) = slot_i * 1.75^level_i. Let vmax = max FaceValue over all non-wild faces. Let q = (#x2)/n, r = (#reroll)/n, w = (#wild)/n, c = (#chain)/n.
  V = Σ over non-wild faces of FaceValue(i)  +  (#wild) * vmax
  B = V / n                      // expected raw face pay
  Dm = (1 - q) / (1 - 2q)        // steady-state Double stacking, q < 0.5
  Rm = 1 / (1 - r)               // expected rolls per tap from Reroll chains
  Cm = 1 + c * (1 + (D - 1) * c) // Chain, D = dice in play
  E[pips per tap, one die] = B * Dm * Rm * Cm * DieMult * GlobalMult * Momentum
Caps enforced by the shop, chosen so the closed forms stay finite and a simulator can assert them: #x2 <= floor(0.40n) (so Dm <= 3.00), #reroll <= floor(0.40n) (Rm <= 1.667), #wild <= floor(n/4), #chain <= floor(n/3).
Sanity values a simulator should reproduce: plain die, all level 0 → B = (n+1)/2. n=1 → 1.0. n=6 → 3.5. n=20 → 10.5.

MULTI-DICE AND HANDS. One tap rolls all D dice. Let m = the largest number of dice showing the same slot number.
  HandMult = 1 + 0.6 * (m - 1)
  E[pips per tap] = HandMult_expected * Σ over dice of E[pips per tap, die k]
For uniform equal-sided dice, E[HandMult] = 1 + 0.6 * (E[m] - 1); at D=2, n sides, P(match) = 1/n so E[HandMult] = 1 + 0.6/n. At D=3, n=10, E[HandMult] ≈ 1.18. Runtime computes m from the actual result array — the closed form is only for balance checking. CHAIN faces additionally pay (1 + (D-1)*c) times their own value, which is what makes a third die worth more than its raw payout.

AUTO-ROLL RATE.
  RollsPerSec(A) = 0.6 * 1.16^(A-1), A >= 1, A = 0 means manual only.
  A=1 → 0.60/s, A=5 → 1.09/s, A=10 → 2.29/s, A=15 → 4.81/s, A=20 → 10.10/s, A=25 → 21.2/s.
  Above 12/s the renderer batches: rolls are still computed individually (max 40 per frame, remainder carried) but drawn as a blur + ticker.
  Manual tap floor: 220 ms between accepted taps (animation is 320 ms and overlaps), so a human peaks near 2.5 rolls/s.
  Momentum = 1 + 0.15 * min(3, taps in the last 3 s), i.e. 1.00 to 1.45.

OFFLINE (closed form, never simulated).
  offlinePips = RollsPerSec(A) * E[pips per tap] * min(elapsedSeconds, CAP) * 0.5
  CAP = 4 h before the first Recast, 8 h after, 12 h with the Shard Shop upgrade. The 0.5 is the offline rate; a rewarded ad raises it to 1.0 and adds 2 h to the cap for that claim only.

PRESTIGE.
  Unlocks at 10 sides on die 1.
  ShardsGained = floor( 8 * (LifetimePips / 1e6) ^ 0.42 )   // 1e6 → 8, 1e8 → 57, 1e10 → 420, 1e12 → 3078
  GlobalMult = 1 + 0.12 * TotalShards                        // linear and legible on purpose
  Recast resets: dice count to 1, sides to 1, all face levels and types, DieMult to 1, and autoLevel to 1 (NOT 0 — automation is monotonic and is never taken away once bought). It keeps: TotalShards, GlobalMult, Shard Shop purchases, LifetimePips, and one player-chosen face (its type and level are written onto the new die's slot 1).

**Cost curves:** SIDE COST (cost to go from n sides to n+1), identical for every die:
  SideCost(n) = round( 25 * 1.95^(n-1) )
  n=1 → 25, 2 → 49, 3 → 95, 4 → 185, 5 → 361, 6 → 705, 7 → 1,374, 8 → 2,680, 9 → 5,226, 10 → 10,190, 11 → 19,871, 12 → 38,748, 14 → 147,315, 16 → 560,100, 18 → 2,129,000, 20 → 8,095,000.
  Growth rate 1.95 per side. Note the deliberate tension: a plain side always adds exactly +0.5 to the expected roll, so raw sides are a shrinking relative gain against an exponential price. Sides stall naturally around n=14-20 per Recast, which is exactly when face upgrades, carves and a second die must take over. Do not 'fix' this; it is the pacing.

FACE UPGRADE COST (slot s, current level L, buying level L+1):
  UpgradeCost(s, L) = round( 15 * s * 3.2^L )
  FaceValue after = s * 1.75^(L+1). Growth rate 3.2 in cost vs 1.75 in value, so a single face saturates in about 6-8 levels and the player is pushed to spread levels across slots — which is the face-builder decision.
  Examples: slot 1 L0 → 15 (value 1 → 1.75). Slot 6 L0 → 90 (6 → 10.5). Slot 6 L3 → 2,949 (32.2 → 56.3). Slot 20 L5 → 100,663 (321 → 562).

CARVE COST (converting a face to an effect type), where k = how many faces of that type the die already has and n = sides:
  CarveCost(type, k, n) = round( TYPE_BASE[type] * 1.60^k * 1.35^(n-1) )
  TYPE_BASE = { x2: 600, reroll: 900, wild: 1500, chain: 2200 }
  Growth 1.60 per copy of the same effect (so stacking one effect gets expensive fast) and 1.35 per side (so carving a big die costs more, keeping carves and sides in competition).
  Example: first x2 on a d6 → 600 * 1.35^5 = 2,762. Second x2 on a d8 → 600 * 1.6 * 1.35^7 = 8,057.
  Un-carving back to 'num' is free, so experimenting is safe and no build is a dead end.

AUTO-ROLLER COST (buying level A):
  AutoCost(A) = round( 2500 * 2.15^(A-1) )
  A=1 → 2,500 (the unlock), A=2 → 5,375, A=5 → 53,404, A=10 → 2.00e6, A=15 → 7.5e7, A=20 → 2.8e9. Growth 2.15 vs a 1.16 rate gain, so auto-roll is a steady drip, never the dominant purchase.

EXTRA DIE COST (buying die number D+1 when you own D):
  DieCost(D) = round( 60000 * 12^(D-1) )
  Second die → 60,000. Third → 720,000. Fourth → 8,640,000. Growth 12. Prototype caps at 4 dice.

SHARD SHOP (flat prices, bought once each): Momentum+ 20 (momentum cap +45% → +75%), Head Start 40 (every Recast begins at d3), Deep Sleep 60 (offline cap 12 h), Face Memory 120 (keep 2 faces through Recast), Kickstart 200 (every Recast begins at auto level 4).

BALANCE TARGET FOR THE SIMULATOR: with a player who taps at 2 rolls/s while the app is open and always buys the cheapest available purchase that raises E[pips per tap] most per Pip spent, cumulative wall-clock time to each milestone should land within ±25% of the milestone table. If side 6 arrives before 3 minutes or after 6, adjust the 1.95 growth rate first, never the 25 base.

**Milestones:** first side bought (d1 → d2) 0.3 min, 6 sides (a normal die) 4 min, auto-roller unlocked 7 min, second die 35 min, first Recast (prestige) 55 min, end of prototype content (d20, 4 dice, all 5 face types, 3 Recasts) 480 min

**Die visual:** SINGLE CANVAS, 2D, top-down. The die is a regular convex polygon centred at (cx, cy) with radius R = 110 px on a 360 px-wide phone layout. THE NUMBER OF EDGES IS THE NUMBER OF SIDES — that is the entire visual thesis, and each edge is one entry in the faces array.\n\nSHAPES BY SIDE COUNT. n=1: not a polygon but a plain disc, "the Blank", a single flat circle with one pip at its centre and a faint seam — it reads as an unfinished object. n=2: a lens/coin — two 180° arcs bulging opposite each other, with "1" on one arc and "2" on the other; it does not spin, it FLIPS (scaleX from 1 to -1 and back, three times, decelerating). n=3: triangle. n=4: square (rotated 45° so a vertex points up, so it reads as a d4). n=5..n=20: regular n-gons, drawn with a 5 px stroke per edge and a subtly darker fill. Past n≈16 the polygon is visibly approaching a circle, which is intentional — the die "fills in" as it matures.\n\nPER-EDGE DRAWING. Each edge is drawn as its own stroked segment with its own colour, so faces are individually readable: plain 'num' faces are cool grey-blue; ×2 is amber; Reroll is teal with a small circular-arrow glyph at the edge midpoint; Wild is violet with a four-point star; Chain is green with two interlocking rings. The face's value is printed just inside its edge midpoint, rotated to sit parallel to that edge, with its upgrade level shown as 0-6 small dots beneath the number. Tapping an edge selects it (edge thickens to 9 px, a radial line is drawn from centre to that edge midpoint) and opens that face's compact upgrade/carve panel under the die. Past 14 sides the numbers shrink to 10 px and migrate to a ring just outside the polygon with tick marks connecting each label to its edge.\n\nROLL ANIMATION (320 ms total, all on one rAF loop, only redrawing while dirty). Phase 1, 0-60 ms: anticipation — the whole polygon squashes to scale(1.08, 0.92) and the result chevron at 12 o'clock flashes white. Phase 2, 60-260 ms: the polygon rotates about its centre by (2.5 full turns + the angle needed to bring the winning edge's midpoint to 12 o'clock), easeOutCubic; fake motion blur by drawing three ghost copies at the previous three frames' angles with alpha 0.25/0.15/0.08. Phase 3, 260-320 ms: overshoot 6° and settle with easeOutBack; the winning edge pulses (stroke 5 → 11 → 6), its colour flashes to the accent, a thin white wedge lights the sector from centre to that edge, and the payout floats up from the edge midpoint as "+N" rising 40 px over 400 ms while fading. A ×2 armed by a Double face draws a persistent amber ring just outside the polygon with the current pending multiplier written in it; a Reroll immediately re-enters phase 2 with no anticipation, so chains feel like one continuous spin.\n\nADDING A SIDE (600 ms, the money shot). Interpolate vertices: the n-gon's vertex i maps to the (n+1)-gon's vertex i, and the new vertex is born at the midpoint of the last edge with radius 0 and is pushed outward to R along an easeOutBack curve while all other vertices slide to their new angles. The new edge is revealed with a stroke-dash sweep from one vertex to the other, its number engraves in with a scale-from-1.6 punch, the screen shakes 4 px for 180 ms, a white ring bursts outward from the centre, and the max-roll readout above the die ticks up. d1→d2 is special-cased: the disc tears into the lens with a hard cut and a heavier shake, because it is the single most important animation in the game.\n\nMULTI-DICE. At D≥2 each die shrinks to R=70 and they lay out in a row (D=2,3) or 2×2 grid (D=4), rolling in staggered 60 ms offsets so the results land one after another like a hand being dealt. If two or more dice land on the same slot number, a glowing line is drawn between those winning edge midpoints and a "MATCH ×1.6" banner slides in above; Chain faces landing together draw their link glyphs joining into a single chain across the dice.\n\nPERFORMANCE / BATTERY. One canvas, requestAnimationFrame, and the loop exits entirely when nothing is animating and no auto-roll is due. At auto-roll rates above 12/s the per-roll animation is replaced by a continuously spinning motion-blurred polygon plus a numeric ticker, and rolls are resolved in batches of up to 40 per frame. When the page is hidden (visibilitychange) all animation stops, no rAF runs, and re-entry pays out with the closed-form offline formula rather than replaying rolls.

**Slot reskin notes:** The engine never knows it is about to become a slot machine, because a reel already IS a die. Keep three boundaries clean from day one. (1) DATA: a face is { slot, type, level } plus a theme-supplied label and glyph. Nothing in the simulation reads the label — payouts come from slot and level, effects from type. To reskin, the theme layer maps slot 1..n to symbols (cherry, bell, bar, seven, abstract shapes of your own design) instead of the numbers 1..n, and maps the five effect types 1:1: ×2 → multiplier symbol, Reroll → respin symbol, Wild → WILD, Chain → scatter. Zero simulation code changes. (2) RENDERER: the only genuinely new code is a strip renderer. Instead of laying the faces array around a polygon, lay it vertically as a scrolling reel strip, and replace the rotate-and-settle animation with a translateY-and-settle using the same easing curve and the same "winning face is highlighted, +N floats up" resolution. Write the renderer behind a single draw(die, ctx) interface from the start so this is one swapped module, not a rewrite. (3) SCORING: the multi-dice hand evaluator is already a payline evaluator. Three dice rolled together with HandMult = 1 + 0.6*(m-1), where m is the largest count of matching slot numbers, is exactly a three-reel one-payline slot. The slot build adds paylines by running the same evaluator over multiple index sets of the result array. The core hook survives intact and is just as unclaimed: your reel starts with ONE symbol on it and you buy symbols one at a time, watching the strip get longer and the machine get more interesting. Ship it as a SEPARATE Play listing, not an in-app mode: declare simulated gambling honestly in IARC and accept Teen/12+, use only original abstract symbols and never real casino brands, marks or trade dress, add an in-app and store-listing disclaimer ("no real-money gambling, no prizes of real-world value, intended for users of legal gambling age"), and add no cash-out, sweepstakes or tournament-with-prize feature of any kind. Expect rewarded-ad eCPM to be worse than the dice app if AdMob classifies the inventory as gambling-related — ship dice first, measure real eCPM for a month, then decide whether the slot version is worth building at all. Add symbols at the same deliberate pace as dice sides; the loudest complaint about the best slot-builder on the market is symbol bloat drowning the strategy.

**Retention hooks:**
- The shape is the progress bar. Competitors show progress as a number on a button; here the object itself re-forms every time you spend. A player can tell at a glance how far they got, and a screenshot of a 17-sided glowing die sells the game with zero marketing copy.
- Every added side produces an immediate, visible, legible payoff: the new number appears in the roll pool within seconds, the max-roll readout ticks up, and the polygon changes silhouette. The known failure mode in this niche is a central die whose purpose players cannot grasp — this design makes each purchase self-explaining.
- Drip-fed reveals, one system at a time, in this order: buy side → upgrade face → carve ×2 → auto-roll → carve Reroll → second die and hands → carve Wild → Recast → carve Chain. Never two new buttons in the same minute, and the whole game stays playable without a wiki.
- Automation is monotonic. Once auto-roll is bought it never turns off, never degrades, and is not taken away by prestige. The single loudest complaint about the genre's sequel is that an 'idle' game stops being idle; this design makes that structurally impossible.
- Recast rebuilds the toy instead of deleting it. You keep one face of your choosing, so the second run starts as a d1 with a Wild on it — an object that has never existed before — and you get to watch the growth animation again, faster.
- Offline earnings are computed with a closed-form formula on resume and capped at 4-12 hours, so coming back after a night is a real, immediate, satisfying number rather than a simulated grind that flattens the battery.
- One die on one screen is a genuine phone-sized advantage. The leader's sequel is widely criticised for UI sprawl across 25 dice and a card layer; this game's entire interface is a polygon and three buttons.
- Phase-two content is already designed into the data model: editing what is ON each face is proven fun in premium PC dicebuilders and has never shipped in a free mobile idle. Because faces are objects from day one, that is a content patch, not a rewrite.

**Monetization hooks:**
- Rewarded ads only. No IAP at all in the prototype, and ideally none ever — standing against a category leader with a $49.99 top SKU is free positioning in the store description and on r/incremental_games, which is the only audience reachable with zero marketing budget.
- Offline doubler: on return, one optional ad raises the offline rate from 0.5 to 1.0 and adds 2 hours to the cap for that claim. Highest-value, lowest-friction placement; it is the only ad the average player will ever see.
- Lucky Streak: 2 minutes of ×2 on all payouts, offered at most once per 15 minutes and capped at 6 per day. Player-initiated from a small persistent button, never a popup.
- Half-Price Side: one 50% discount on the next BUY SIDE, offered once per side tier. It targets exactly the moment the exponential curve starts to bite, which is the moment a player would otherwise close the app.
- Auto-Bank: skip 30 minutes of auto-roll earnings ahead, once per 2 hours. Uses the same closed-form offline formula, so it costs nothing to implement.
- HARD ENGINEERING RULE, learned from the leader's 4.0 iOS rating versus 4.7 on Android: grant the reward LOCALLY AND IDEMPOTENTLY the instant the player presses the button, with a 4-second timeout on the ad SDK, and grant it anyway on failure, error, no-fill, or offline. Never block progression, never show an interstitial, never freeze the UI waiting for an SDK. An ad that fails must cost the player nothing.
- No randomized purchases anywhere. Every price is fixed and displayed, so Play's loot-box odds disclosure requirement never applies and the IARC gambling questions can all be answered 'no' honestly for the dice version.
- Declare a 13+ target audience and keep the art geometric and clean rather than cartoon-mascot cute. This keeps the app out of Families policy scope, keeps every ad SDK available, and avoids a declared-audience mismatch, which is a suspension ground rather than a rating correction.

**Risks:**
- Plain sides give a fixed +0.5 expected roll while their cost grows 1.95× per side, so raw side-buying stalls hard around n=14-20. If face upgrades, carves, extra dice and Recast are not tuned to take over at exactly that point, the game flatlines in the second hour. This is the single most important thing for a simulator to check.
- The Double face's steady-state multiplier (1-q)/(1-2q) diverges as q approaches 0.5, and Reroll's 1/(1-r) diverges at r=1. The 0.40n caps are load-bearing, not cosmetic — remove them and the economy explodes or the game locks into an infinite reroll chain.
- Reroll chains are recursive. A naive implementation will blow the JS stack or hang a frame on a long chain; it must be an iterative loop with a hard cap of 50 chained rolls per tap, and at high auto-roll rates chains must be resolved by the closed-form 1/(1-r) multiplier rather than actually rolled.
- Big-number overflow. Payouts pass 1e15 within a few Recasts and plain JS numbers lose integer precision, then hit Infinity. Either cap prototype content below 1e15 (the milestone table does) or use a mantissa/exponent pair from day one. Retrofitting this later means touching every formula.
- Battery and thermals are the top recurring complaint in this exact niche (~20% per hour for the leader). Continuous canvas animation plus a physics-flavoured roll will reproduce it. The blur-and-ticker mode above 12 rolls/s, the rAF exit when idle, and the visibilitychange stop are mandatory, not polish.
- Rewarded-ad reliability is the difference between a 4.7 and a 4.0. If the Capacitor ad plugin can ever swallow a reward or freeze the WebView after an ad, the store rating is capped no matter how good the game is. Grant locally first, always.
- Realistic ceiling. A dice idle with varied side counts and unremarkable execution earned about 5.6k downloads; the leader took years to reach 500k+. A few hundred dollars a month from rewarded ads needs tens of thousands of engaged installs. The first five minutes and the store screenshots are effectively the entire marketing budget, so disproportionate effort belongs on the d1→d6 transitions.
- Scope creep toward the competitor's mistakes. Cards, duels, a second currency, a roulette mini-game and a research tree are all tempting and all fatal here — the differentiation is one die on one screen, and every added system dilutes the only thing that is unclaimed.
- The slot reskin may be a trap. It costs a Teen/12+ rating, risks AdMob classifying the inventory as gambling-related (which measurably reduces or eliminates fill), and needs Google Ads social-casino certification if ever advertised. It should be gated behind measured eCPM from the dice app, not built on faith.
- A single HTML file at 2,500 lines with no build step and no tests is hard to refactor safely. The faces-array-as-single-source-of-truth discipline is what keeps it tractable; if game state starts living in DOM attributes or in the renderer, the slot reskin becomes a rewrite.

**Out of scope:**
- Anything beyond 4 dice, 20 sides, 5 face types, 3 Recasts and 5 Shard Shop entries. That is the prototype; everything else is a later content patch.
- Per-face custom art, face-swapping between dice, face merging/combining, and negative or cursed faces. The data model supports them; the prototype ships four effect types plus plain.
- Achievements, statistics screens, a lifetime-records page, a changelog, a settings menu beyond mute and a hard reset.
- Cloud saves, accounts, leaderboards, any server component at all. Save is a single JSON blob in localStorage, written on a 5-second debounce and on visibilitychange, with a version integer for migrations.
- Sound design beyond four short WebAudio-synthesised blips (roll, land, purchase, side-added) generated in code. No audio files, no music.
- 3D or WebGL rendering, real dice physics, and any physics library. The roll is a rotation with easing; it must never simulate tumbling.
- Big-number libraries, state-management frameworks, build tooling, TypeScript, npm dependencies of any kind. One file, no imports.
- The slot mode itself. Build only the seam: faces as data, a draw(die, ctx) renderer interface, a theme object holding every user-visible string and glyph, and the hand evaluator written over a result array rather than over two named dice.
- The actual AdMob integration, Capacitor wrapper, and Play Console listing. The prototype stubs the ad call as a function that resolves true after 300 ms so the reward path and its failure path are both exercisable in the browser.
- Localisation, accessibility beyond legible contrast and 44 px touch targets, tablet and desktop layouts. Portrait phone only.
- Balance tuning past the milestone table. Ship with these constants, play it on a phone for an evening, and adjust only the 1.95 side-cost growth rate and the 60,000 second-die price before touching anything else.
- Any randomized or paid-for-randomness purchase, any currency conversion, cash-out, gifting, sweepstakes or prize mechanic. These are permanently out of scope, not merely deferred — they are what keeps the app outside gambling policy entirely.

## Design 1: One-Sided
**Hook:** Start with a die that has exactly one side; buy the second side, then the third, and watch the shape itself become your progress bar.

**Core loop:** Tap the die -> it tumbles along a ground line and lands on an edge -> the number on that edge pops up and becomes coins -> one big button says "+ SIDE" with the price on it -> tap it -> the polygon visibly grows an edge with a gold flash and screen shake, and the new edge carries a bigger number than any before it. Repeat. Three other buttons appear over time and they are the only other things in the game: FILE (raise the die's lowest face by 1), ROLLER (roll automatically, faster), + DIE (a second die, born at one side again). Everything is a number on a button and a shape on the screen. No menus, no tabs, no reading, one thumb, everything reachable in the bottom third of the screen.

**First five minutes:** 0:00 - The screen holds one object: a white ball with a single flat spot resting on a line, and the word-free prompt of a pulsing finger icon. Tap. It rolls one pivot, lands on its flat face, and a "+1" pops off it. Coins: 1.
0:06 - After the 4th roll a button slides up from the bottom: a polygon icon with a +, and "10". The player has 6-8 coins and can see it filling with a progress ring. Two more taps.
0:10-0:15 - FIRST PURCHASE. The ball squashes, splits into a lens/coin shape, a "2" stamps onto the new edge, screen shake, coin burst, a low chunky thunk. The die now sometimes rolls 2. The button instantly re-prices to 20. This whole beat is the store screenshot.
0:15-0:40 - Sides 3 and 4 arrive roughly 11s and 17s apart. The die becomes a triangle, then a square. Each new edge carries a visibly larger numeral (1,2,3,4). Around side 3 a second small button appears: FILE, 12 coins, which turns the die's worst face from 1 into 2. It is cheap enough to buy on impulse and its effect is visible on the shape, so it teaches "faces have values" with no text.
0:40-1:40 - Sides 5 and 6. Face values start outrunning side count (side 5 is worth 6, side 6 is worth 9), so payouts jump noticeably and the pentagon/hexagon read as a real die for the first time. At 6 sides the die is a familiar hexagon and the game has taught itself entirely.
1:40-3:00 - The ROLLER button appears (600). Player mashes toward it, buys side 7 (worth 14) on the way. Buying ROLLER makes the die start rolling on its own at 2 rolls/sec, faster than comfortable tapping, and the player's hands come off the phone for the first time. Tapping still adds an extra throw, so mashing is never punished.
3:00-5:00 - Sides 8 and 9 (worth 22 and 34) and ROLLER level 2. The die is a nonagon rolling by itself, numbers streaming off it. The + DIE button is visible but greyed with its unlock condition shown as a ghosted 10-sided polygon, which is the next thing the player wants.

**Systems:**
- **The Die (faces as data):** A die is {faces: [int], autoRate shared globally}. faces.length is the side count S. Rolling picks a uniform random index i in [0,S) and pays faces[i]. That is the entire simulation. Faces are a plain array of integers from the first line of code so that per-face content (and slot symbols) is a data change, never a rewrite.
- **+ SIDE:** Appends a new face whose value is V(n) = ceil(1.55^(n-1)) for the n-th side. The value ladder is 1,2,3,4,6,9,14,22,34,52,81,125,193,299,463,717,1110,1721,2667,4134. Because each new face beats every old one, adding a side both raises the ceiling and drags up the average. With multiple dice the button always targets the die with the fewest sides (highlighted with a pulse), so there is never a choice to make.
- **FILE:** Raises the lowest-valued face by +1 (ties: lowest index). Adds exactly 1/S to expected value. It is the cheap impulse button that keeps the early game clicking and quietly teaches that faces carry values. It is deliberately a fading system: it auto-hides once its payback time exceeds 3x the + SIDE payback time (roughly side 11), keeping the screen clean.
- **ROLLER:** Automation, and it is monotonic forever: once bought it never stops, never needs re-tapping, never gets gated. 9 levels, global across all dice (one shake of the cup). Manual taps still fire an extra throw with a 100ms cooldown, so the tap is never dead.
- **+ DIE:** Unlocks at 10 sides on the first die. A new die is born at one side, and because + SIDE auto-targets the smallest die, the player immediately gets to watch a die grow from a ball to a decagon in about two minutes with late-game income behind it. This is the single best-feeling stretch in the game and it is free content, reusing the same button.
- **THROW and COMBO:** All dice are thrown together in one animation. Payout = (sum of the landed face values) x comboMult x prestigeMult. comboMult is driven by the largest group of equal landed values: 1 die or no match = x1, pair = x2, three = x5, four = x15, five = x50. Matching dice flash and connect with a light thread; the multiplier pops in the middle of the throw. Because every die draws from the same V(n) ladder, matches are common and juicy when dice are similar in size and become a rare jackpot once they diverge.
- **RECAST (prestige):** Melts every die back to a single one-sided ball, clears sides, files and roller, and pays out permanent shards. Nothing is kept except the multiplier, on purpose: the reward for prestige is getting to watch the shape grow again, faster. Unlocks the first time a run has earned 250,000 coins.
- **Offline earnings:** Computed in closed form on resume (never simulated), at 50% rate, capped at 8 hours. Shown as a single card with a big COLLECT and a smaller rewarded-ad DOUBLE. This also removes the battery-drain failure mode that plagues the category leader: rolling animation is suspended entirely on visibilitychange.
- **Save / render seam:** State is a single JSON object in localStorage, written every 5s and on visibilitychange, with a version field. All drawing goes through one THEME object ({drawDie, faceLabel, palette, strings}) so the slot build swaps that object and nothing else.

**Economy, currency flow:** ONE currency: coins. Source: throws. Sink: the four buttons. No second currency, no premium currency, no cash-out, no conversion, no gifting - which is exactly what keeps the app outside Play's Real-Money Gambling policy and keeps every upgrade deterministic so no loot-box odds disclosure is triggered.

PER-THROW PAYOUT (the whole economy in one line):
  payout = ( SUM over dice d of faces_d[ randIndex_d ] ) * comboMult(m) * M
where randIndex_d is uniform over [0, S_d), m is the size of the largest group of equal landed values across the throw, and M is the prestige multiplier.

FACE VALUE LADDER (identical for every die):
  V(n) = ceil(1.55^(n-1))  ->  1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 14, 22, 34, 52, 81, 125, 193, 299, 463, 717, 1110, 1721, 2667, 4134
A die with S sides and no FILE purchases has faces [V(1)..V(S)].

EXPECTED VALUE OF ONE DIE:
  E(S) = (1/S) * SUM_{n=1..S} V(n)   (+ 1/S per FILE applied to that die)
  E(1..20) = 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3.2, 4.17, 5.57, 7.63, 10.6, 14.7, 20.7, 29.4, 42.0, 60.4, 87.2, 126.6, 184.4, 269.8, 396, 583

COMBO MULTIPLIER (m = largest equal-value group in the throw):
  m <= 1 -> 1 ; m = 2 -> 2 ; m = 3 -> 5 ; m = 4 -> 15 ; m = 5 -> 50
Measured average combo multiplier on default face sets, for the simulator to re-verify: c_hat = 1.00 (1 die), ~1.35 (2 dice), ~1.8 (3), ~2.4 (4), ~3.2 (5). It rises when dice are close in size and falls as they diverge - that is intended.

INCOME RATE:
  coins_per_sec = throwRate * ( SUM_d E(S_d) ) * c_hat * M
  throwRate = autoRate(L) + manualTaps_per_sec   (manual taps capped at 10/s by a 100ms cooldown)

AUTO-ROLL:
  autoRate(L) = 2.0 * 1.35^L  throws/sec, L = 0..8
  -> 2.0, 2.70, 3.65, 4.92, 6.64, 8.97, 12.1, 16.3, 22.1
Level 0 is deliberately faster than comfortable tapping (~1.7/s) so automation always feels like a gain. Above 8 throws/sec the renderer batches: rolls are still resolved individually for RNG and combos, but drawn in groups of ceil(rate/8) per animation frame with the die in continuous blur.

OFFLINE (closed form, never simulated):
  offlineCoins = 0.5 * autoRate(L) * (SUM_d E(S_d)) * c_hat * M * min(secondsAway, 28800)

PRESTIGE:
  shardsGained = floor( 10 * sqrt( coinsEarnedThisRun / 250000 ) )
  M = 1 + 0.10 * totalShards
  RECAST unlocks when coinsEarnedThisRun >= 250,000 (i.e. the first recast is always worth at least +100%). Typical: run 1 ~250k -> 10 shards -> M=2.0; run 2 ~1M -> 20 more -> M=4.0; run 3 ~4M -> 40 more -> M=8.0.

**Cost curves:** + SIDE (buying the n-th side of die d, n >= 2):
  C_side(d, n) = ceil( 10 * 1.95^(n-2) * 4^(d-1) )
  Die 1: 10, 20, 38, 74, 145, 282, 550, 1073, 2092, 4080, 7956, 15515, 30254, 58995, 115040, 224329, 437441, 853010, 1663370  (sides 2..20)
  Die 2 costs 4x that ladder, die 3 16x, die 4 64x, die 5 256x. Because a new die starts at one side, its early sides are pocket change at the income level where it unlocks - that is the intended catch-up rush.
  Income per side purchase grows ~1.55x while cost grows 1.95x, so each side takes ~1.26x longer than the last. Time per side is ~6s early, ~40s at side 7, ~2.5min at side 14, ~10min at side 19 - which is exactly when RECAST should be pulling instead.

FILE (k = total files bought this run, k starts at 0):
  C_file(k) = ceil( 12 * 1.32^k )  ->  12, 16, 21, 27, 36, 47, 62, 82, 108, 143, 188, 249, ...
  Effect: lowest face +1, i.e. +1/S expected value on that die. Auto-hides when payback(FILE) > 3 * payback(+SIDE).

ROLLER (level L -> L+1):
  C_roller(L) = ceil( 600 * 3.2^L )  ->  600, 1920, 6144, 19661, 62915, 201327, 644245, 2061584, 6597070
  Unlocks at 6 sides on die 1.

+ DIE (buying the (N+1)-th die):
  C_die(N) = 5000 * 12^(N-1)  ->  5000, 60000, 720000, 8640000
  Unlocks at 10 sides on die 1. Hard cap 5 dice in the prototype.

NUMBER FORMATTING: plain up to 9999, then K, M, B, T, then aa, ab, ac... Two significant decimals.
RNG: Math.random is fine; the landed face index is chosen FIRST, and the animation's pivot count is then selected to land on it.

**Milestones:** First side bought (d1 -> d2) 0.25 min, Six sides (the hexagon, game fully taught) 2.5 min, Auto-roller bought (hands off the phone) 4 min, Second die (born at one side, grows in 2 min) 9 min, First RECAST (250k earned, M x2) 30 min, End of prototype content (5 dice, d20, 3rd prestige) 150 min

**Die visual:** RENDERER: one HTML canvas, 2D context, devicePixelRatio-scaled, one requestAnimationFrame loop, suspended entirely on visibilitychange. No physics engine, no 3D. Everything is a polygon path plus text.\n\nTHE OBJECT: the die is drawn as a regular S-sided polygon resting on a horizontal ground line, seen edge-on. Its circumradius R is fixed (~110px on a phone, shrinking to fit when there are multiple dice, min 44px) so the die always occupies the same visual space no matter the side count - only its EDGES change. Vertices are at angle theta_k = -PI/2 + (2PI/S)*k + rotation, and the polygon is rotated so one full edge lies flat on the ground. Each face's numeral is drawn just inside its own edge, rotated to sit parallel to that edge, at ~0.62*R from centre, in a tabular-figures font. So the die literally wears its face values, and the newest face is the biggest number on it.\n\nSHAPE BY SIDE COUNT:\n  S=1: a circle of radius R with a single chord sliced off the bottom - a ball with one flat spot. It reads instantly as \"a die that only has one side\". It can only ever land on that flat.\n  S=2: a lens - two arcs meeting at two points, like a coin seen edge-on. It flips end over end.\n  S=3: triangle. S=4: square. S=5: pentagon. S=6: the familiar hexagon (which is the moment the object finally reads as \"a die\").\n  S=7..12: heptagon through dodecagon; edges shrink, numerals shrink with them (font size = clamp(9, edgeLength*0.42, 34)).\n  S=13..20: approaching a circle; once edge length drops below ~18px the numerals are drawn only on the 4 edges nearest the contact point (the rest become small tick marks), so the shape stays clean and the landed face is always legible.\n  Corners round slightly with S (cornerRadius = 2 + S*0.35) so higher dice look machined and heavy rather than jagged.\n\nADDING A SIDE (the loudest event in the game): over 420ms, lerp every vertex from the S-gon position to the (S+1)-gon position with an ease-out-back curve, while the new edge draws itself in with a gold stroke that flares to 3px and fades. The new numeral stamps in at scale 2.2 -> 1.0 with a 120ms overshoot. Simultaneously: 6px screen shake, a radial burst of ~20 coin particles, a low thunk plus a rising chime, and the whole die flashes to the accent colour and back. This animation is the product.\n\nROLLING: the landed face index is chosen by RNG first. The die then rolls RIGHT along the ground by k pivots, where k is the smallest integer >= 2 satisfying (currentContactFace - k) mod S == targetFace, plus S if k < 2; k is capped at S+4 so big dice do not spin forever. Each pivot is a rotation of exactly the exterior angle 2PI/S about the leading (front-bottom) vertex, with per-pivot duration easing from 150ms down to 90ms and back up to 220ms on the last one (the settle). Between pivots the centre translates automatically because rotation is about the contact vertex - no separate position code. The ground line scrolls left to keep the die centred, with a faint dotted texture so the motion is readable.\n\nSETTLE AND POP: on landing, squash the die to scaleY 0.93 / scaleX 1.05 for 90ms and bounce back; emit 4-6 dust specks from the contact edge; the contact edge flashes white for 60ms. The rolled value floats up from the contact edge, scaling 0.6 -> 1.15 -> 1.0, drifting 70px up over 700ms while fading, coloured by tier (white, then cyan above 100, then gold above 10k, then a thicker outlined style above 1M). The coin counter ticks up with a 180ms lerp rather than snapping.\n\nMULTIPLE DICE: dice sit in a row on the same ground line and all pivot simultaneously with a 40ms stagger so the throw sounds like a handful, not a metronome. Matching dice get a 2px glow ring and a thin light thread drawn between their centres; the combo multiplier pops in the centre of the screen at 1.6x scale before the coin total resolves.\n\nHIGH SPEED: above 8 throws/sec the pivot animation is replaced by a continuous blur - the polygon vibrates within a 4px radius, its outline doubled at low alpha, and value pops are emitted as a stream (one drawn per frame, the rest merged into the counter). Reverts to full tumbling if the rate ever drops.\n\nIDLE: when untouched and not auto-rolling for 3s, the die breathes (scale 1.0 -> 1.012, 2.4s sine) and a faint finger icon fades in. Nothing else on screen moves.

**Slot reskin notes:** The seam is already the data model, so the slot build is a theme swap plus a store listing, not a fork.\n\nWHAT IS ALREADY SHARED: a die is {faces: [{value, label}]}; a reel is exactly that with symbol labels instead of numerals. A throw is \"roll N dice simultaneously, collect the landed face array\"; a spin is the identical call with N reels. The combo evaluator already takes the landed value array and returns the largest matching group - that IS a payline evaluator for a single centre line. + SIDE becomes + SYMBOL on the same C_side ladder and the same auto-targeting rule (always the reel with the fewest symbols), so \"a reel that starts with one symbol and grows\" is the identical hook with zero new systems. ROLLER becomes SPIN SPEED unchanged. + DIE becomes + REEL unchanged. RECAST becomes REBUILD unchanged. Every formula and constant in the economy section carries over untouched.\n\nWHAT HAS TO BE WRITTEN: a single THEME object - THEME = {drawDie(ctx, die, state), faceLabel(face), palette, strings, currencyName, sounds}. The dice theme draws a rolling polygon; the slot theme draws a vertical strip of symbol cells that scrolls and decelerates to the pre-chosen face index (same RNG-first, animation-second rule; the deceleration curve replaces the pivot sequence, and the settle bounce and number pop are reused verbatim). Multi-reel stagger replaces multi-die stagger. Roughly 150-250 lines.\n\nWHAT MUST NOT CHANGE, FOR POLICY: no bet sizing, no wager, no stake, no cash-out, no sweepstakes, no gift cards, no real casino brand names, logos or trade dress. Spins stay free and every upgrade stays deterministically priced in soft currency, which keeps loot-box odds disclosure out of scope. Ship it as a SEPARATE Play listing sharing the engine, declare simulated gambling honestly in IARC (expect Teen/12+), add the disclaimer string to both the app and the listing, and use abstract original symbols. Ship the dice app first and measure real rewarded-ad eCPM before investing in the slot build, because AdMob may classify slot inventory as gambling-related and cut fill - that is the only real reason the slot version is phase two rather than a mode inside phase one.\n\nSYMBOL PACING: start the reel with ONE symbol and add them one at a time on the same minutes-apart curve as sides. Do not exceed ~20 symbols in the prototype.

**Retention hooks:**
- Offline earnings card on every launch: one line, one big COLLECT, closed-form maths, capped at 8 hours at 50% rate. It is the whole reason to reopen the app.
- The + SIDE button carries a progress ring that fills as coins accumulate, so the next shape change is always visible on screen without any text.
- Daily recast bonus: first launch after 20h grants one free + SIDE on the smallest die, applied with the full growth animation. The reward is the animation, not the coins.
- Ghosted previews: the locked + DIE button shows a faint outline of the polygon you would get; the next face value is drawn greyed on the die's imaginary next edge. The player can always see the shape they are working toward.
- RECAST is framed as "melt it down and grow it again, faster" - the reward is re-watching the shape evolve with a multiplier, not a number going up on a menu screen.
- Automation is monotonic by rule: once ROLLER is bought it never stops, never needs re-tapping and is never gated behind an ad or a wall. The single loudest complaint about the category leader's sequel is that the idle game stops being idle.
- Battery honesty: animation suspends completely when backgrounded and earnings are computed in closed form on resume. The leader is repeatedly reviewed at ~20% battery per hour; not doing that is a review-score feature.

**Monetization hooks:**
- Rewarded ad: DOUBLE offline earnings, offered once per launch on the collect card. Highest-intent placement in the game.
- Rewarded ad: HOT HAND - x3 payouts for 2 minutes, available once every 20 minutes, surfaced as a small flame button that never covers the die.
- Rewarded ad: SHORTFALL - when the player is within 20% of affording a + SIDE, an optional button grants 4 minutes of current income. Once per side, never required.
- Hard rule learned from the leader's iOS collapse (4.0 vs 4.7 on Android over an ads update that froze the game and withheld bonuses): every ad call is wrapped in a 6s timeout, the reward is granted LOCALLY and IDEMPOTENTLY by a transaction id whether the SDK succeeds, fails, or never returns, and the game state is saved before the ad is requested. A failed ad still pays out.
- No interstitials, no banners over the play area, no forced ads, no IAP in the prototype. The store description can honestly say "no purchases, ads optional" - which is free positioning against a leader whose top SKU is $49.99, and the only pitch that works on r/incremental_games without a marketing budget.
- Later (not prototype): a single $2.99 tip/thank-you IAP that grants a cosmetic die material and nothing mechanical. Keeps coins one-way and terminal so the Real-Money Gambling policy never applies.

**Risks:**
- The whole product rests on one animation. If the d1 -> d2 -> d3 transitions do not feel chunky and physical, there is no game and no screenshot. Budget disproportionate effort there and prototype that transition before anything else; if it is not satisfying with placeholder art, stop.
- Manual tapping outperforming the first ROLLER level would poison the automation moment. Constants are set so autoRate(0)=2.0/s beats comfortable tapping (~1.7/s), but this must be checked on a real phone with a real thumb, not in a desktop browser.
- Side count and cost growth are tuned analytically (income x1.55 per side vs cost x1.95). A simulator should verify time-per-side stays between 6s and 10min across the whole run and that no purchase is ever strictly dominated. If the mid-game stalls around sides 12-15, lower the side growth rate to 1.90 rather than adding a new system.
- FILE is a deliberately fading button. If playtesters buy it long past usefulness and feel cheated, replace the auto-hide with a hard stop at side 11 rather than buffing it.
- Combo multipliers are the one place with real variance; with 5 dice a x50 can dwarf several minutes of income and make the curve lumpy. Cap combo contribution at 40% of measured income in the simulator, or drop the m=5 tier to x30.
- Legibility failure is the known killer in this niche (an obscure dice incremental died on "I could not grasp what the big die meant"). Every purchase must produce an immediate visible change ON THE DIE. If any upgrade only changes a number in the HUD, cut it.
- Numbers exceed Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER around 3-4 prestiges deep if content is extended. Prototype is safe in doubles, but any content past ~1e15 needs a mantissa/exponent number type - a rewrite risk if deferred too long. Keep all currency arithmetic behind add/mul/cmp helper functions from line one so the swap is mechanical.
- Art style is a policy surface, not just taste: keep it geometric, matte, dark-background, no cartoon mascot, no nursery palette, and declare a 13+ audience so Families policy never applies and all ad SDKs stay available.
- One self-contained HTML file at ~2000 lines is achievable only if the scope list below is respected. The most likely failure mode is scope creep into achievements, settings and cosmetics before the core loop is proven.

**Out of scope:**
- Per-face editing, face swapping, special faces (re-roll faces, multiplier faces, wild faces) - this is the phase-two content patch, and it is why faces are an array of objects from day one. Not in the prototype.
- Keeping a face through RECAST. Tempting and on-theme, but it breaks the early curve and the joy of regrowing. Revisit after the first prestige loop is measured.
- The slot reskin itself. Build the seam (THEME object, faces-as-data, matching-group evaluator), build none of the slot.
- Cards, decks, roulette, duels, quests, events, a second currency, skill trees, or any system not named in the owner's concept.
- More than 5 dice, more than 20 sides, or more than 3 prestige layers.
- 3D dice, physics engines, WebGL, imported libraries of any kind. Canvas 2D only, zero dependencies.
- Achievements grid, statistics screen, leaderboards, cloud save, accounts, social features.
- Settings beyond a mute toggle and a hold-to-confirm hard reset. No options menu, no language selection, no tutorial text.
- Localization. The game is designed to have almost no words; keep it that way rather than translating it.
- IAP, shop, currency packs, battle pass, subscriptions.
- Capacitor wrapping, AdMob integration and Play Console work - these come after the single HTML file is fun on a phone in the browser. Stub the ad calls behind a grantReward(id, amount) function that currently just grants.

## Design 2: One More Side
**Hook:** You start with a one-sided die. Every coin you earn buys it another side — and you watch the shape physically grow from a blank stone to a 20-faceted jewel.

**Core loop:** Tap the die → it tumbles and lands on a face → that face's value is paid in coins → spend coins on the one thing that matters (another SIDE) or on the small sinks between sides (sharpen a face's pips, speed up the auto-roller) → the die visibly re-forms with one more facet, the max possible roll goes up, and the next side costs ~2.9x more. Automation arrives early and never leaves. When side costs outrun a single die, you buy a permanent second die slot; two dice rolled together add combo payouts. When even that stalls, you Recast: melt the die back to one side for permanent shards and the pleasure of watching it grow again, faster.

Everything in the game resolves to one expression the player can feel: payout = value(face you landed on) x combo x global multipliers. Nothing else is bolted on.

**First five minutes:** 0:00 — Screen is a dark slate with one pale circular stone in the middle, a single pip on it, and the word TAP pulsing under it. No menus, no tutorial text, no popup. Coin counter reads 0.
0:00–0:12 — Every tap tumbles the stone and pays exactly 1 coin (a d1 can only roll 1). This is deliberately monotonous for about ten seconds. At 25 coins a single button slides up from the bottom: "ADD A SIDE — 25". It is the only button on screen.
0:12 — First purchase. The stone stretches from a circle into a lens/coin shape over 600ms with an overshoot, splits into two facets labelled 1 and 3, and lands with a thud. Next tap can now pay 3. This is the entire pitch delivered in twelve seconds and it is also screenshot #1 on the store listing.
0:12–0:30 — Rolls now average 2. Side 3 costs 73; it lands around 0:29. The lens snaps into a triangle. A thin "MAX ROLL 6" readout appears under the die and ticks up with every side — the legibility fix for the "I couldn't tell what the big die did" failure mode.
0:30–1:00 — Side 4 (210) turns the triangle into a diamond/tetra silhouette around 0:58. A second button appears: "BIND THE DIE — 400" (the auto-roller).
1:00–2:30 — Player buys the auto-roller (~1:40 if they beeline). The die starts tumbling on its own at 0.5 rolls/sec and a small ring gauge fills around it between rolls. From this moment the game is playable hands-off forever; tapping only ever adds rolls on top, never becomes mandatory.
2:30–4:00 — Side 5 (610). First PIP purchase is offered: "SHARPEN FACE 5 — 195", a cheap, repeatable sink that makes one facet worth more. Two sinks now compete, which is the first real decision.
4:00–5:00 — Auto-roll speed levels are cheap here (648, 1050, 1700) and each one visibly speeds the tumble. Around 4:30 a non-blocking chip slides in from the edge: "LUCKY STREAK — watch a short ad for x3 coins for 3 minutes". It can be dismissed with one tap and re-offered later. That is the first and only ad surface in the first session.
5:00 — Player is at d5 going on d6, roughly 2,500 lifetime coins, a die that has changed shape four times in five minutes, and zero interstitials seen.

**Systems:**
- **The Die (faces as data):** A die is {sides:N, faces:[{index, base, pip}], rolls}. faces[k].base = round(k^1.6) (table: 1,3,6,9,13,18,23,28,34,40,46,53,61,68,76,84,93,102,111,121 for k=1..20; beyond 20 compute the formula). faces[k].pip is an integer upgrade level. Value of a face = base * 1.18^pip. Buying a side appends face N+1 at pip 0. This array-of-face-objects shape is non-negotiable from day one: it is what makes per-face editing a later content patch instead of a rewrite, and what makes the slot reskin a label swap.
- **Rolling:** A roll picks a uniform random face index per die, sums the face values across all active dice, applies the combo multiplier over the set of rolled indices, then applies global multipliers. Manual taps and auto-rolls go through exactly the same function, so nothing can ever be tap-only. Manual taps are capped at ~2.2/sec by the 380ms animation but are queued, not dropped, so fast tapping is never punished.
- **Sides (the milestone sink):** The headline purchase. Always the top button, always labelled with what it unlocks ('ADD A SIDE — max roll 18 → 23'). Costs 25 * 2.9^(N-2) coins for the Nth side. Sides are chunky and rare by design: roughly 12s, 17s, 30s, 50s, 90s, 3m, 6m apart early, then one every 10-25 minutes. Every side triggers the morph animation and a 2-second 'the die is different now' beat with no other UI moving.
- **Pips (the grind sink):** Per-face value upgrades, the fine-grained thing to spend on between sides. costPip(k, p) = 15 * base(k) * 1.26^p, granting 1.18x to that face. Cost grows faster than value (1.26 vs 1.18), so it is a self-limiting infinite sink with no runaway. A 'SHARPEN ALL' bulk button (sum of all faces' next level) unlocks at d8 as pure QoL. Pips are also the only thing the player loses on Recast, which is what makes Recast feel like a choice.
- **Auto-roller (monotonic automation):** Unlocked once for 400 coins at d4-ish. rollsPerSec(L) = 0.5 * 1.16^L, costAuto(L) = 400 * 1.62^L. Once bought it is NEVER removed, gated, re-locked, or fueled — the single loudest complaint about the genre leader's sequel is that the idle layer evaporates, and this game structurally cannot do that. At L=25 the rate hits 20 rolls/sec and the visual cap; every level past 25 converts into a flat x1.16 income multiplier instead of more rolls, which keeps the die legible and the phone cool.
- **Multiple dice & combos:** A second die SLOT is a permanent one-time purchase (400,000 coins, requires die 1 at 10 sides); the slot survives Recast even though the die inside it resets to d1. Each die is fully independent — its own sides, its own pips, its own growth story. Combo multiplier over the rolled index set: all indices equal → (2 + D)x; all indices form a consecutive run → (1 + 0.6*D)x; otherwise 1x. Third die slot at 36,000,000 coins. Prototype ships three slots and stops.
- **Recast (prestige):** Unlocked at 2,000,000 coins earned in the current run. Melts every die back to 1 side, wipes coins and pips, and pays permanent Shards. Keeps: shards, achievements, daily tokens, the second/third die slots, half your auto-roll levels, and one Heirloom Face of your choosing (that face returns at its full pip level as soon as the die has that many sides again). Re-watching the die grow is the reward, not the punishment — which is why the reset is total but the ramp back is ~4x faster.
- **Rewarded ads layer:** Five placements, all opt-in, all non-blocking, all granted LOCALLY AND IDEMPOTENTLY: the game writes a pending-reward record, calls the SDK with a 12-second timeout, and grants the reward on success, failure, timeout, or no-fill alike. The genre leader's iOS rating is a full point below its Android rating specifically because an ads update froze the game and withheld promised bonuses. Never block a purchase, a side, or a Recast behind an ad.
- **Offline progress (closed form, no simulation):** On resume: coins += 0.5 * rollsPerSec * E[payout] * min(elapsedSeconds, 14400). E[payout] is recomputed and cached with a 4,000-sample Monte Carlo (~1ms) whenever the build changes — never by simulating rolls. Cap is 4 hours, extendable to 8 and doubled to 100% rate by one rewarded ad on the welcome-back card. This also solves the battery complaint that dogs the incumbent: no physics, no background timer, and the render loop drops to 0 fps when the page is hidden.
- **Daily Die & streaks:** One free ceremonial roll per calendar day on a gold die, paying 20 minutes of current income times a streak multiplier (day 1-7: 1.0, 1.15, 1.3, 1.5, 1.75, 2.0, 3.0). A 7-day streak also mints one Token worth a permanent +2% global multiplier, capped at 20 tokens. Streak breaks are forgiven once per 7 days automatically, silently — punishing a lapse is how you lose the players you already have.
- **Theme layer (the slot seam):** All presentation goes through one THEME object: shape renderer, faceLabel(k), currency name, palette, sfx set, and every user-facing string. The simulation layer never contains the word 'dice' or 'slot'. Swapping THEME is the entire difference between the two products, plus store listing and IARC answers.
- **Achievements (12, flat):** Twelve one-line achievements, each +5% permanent global multiplier (+60% total). They exist to name the milestones ('Six Sides', 'Bound the Die', 'First Recast', 'Twenty Faces') and to give the player something to read on the pause screen. No tiers, no meta-currency, no second screen.

**Economy, currency flow:** ONE currency: Coins. Earned only by rolling. Spent only on sides, pips, auto-roll levels, and die slots. Never purchasable with real money, never redeemable, never transferable — this single rule keeps the app entirely outside Play's Real-Money Gambling policy.

FACE VALUES
base(k) = round(k^1.6). Precomputed table for k=1..20: [1,3,6,9,13,18,23,28,34,40,46,53,61,68,76,84,93,102,111,121].
value(k) = base(k) * 1.18^pip[k]     // pip[k] is an integer >= 0, starts at 0

INCOME PER ROLL (single die, N sides)
A roll picks index k uniform in [1..N].
payout = value(k) * combo * G
E[payout | 1 die] = ( (1/N) * SUM_{k=1..N} base(k)*1.18^pip[k] ) * G
With all pips at 0, E collapses to EV(N) = (1/N)*SUM base(k). Reference values: EV(1)=1, EV(2)=2, EV(3)=3.33, EV(4)=4.75, EV(5)=6.40, EV(6)=8.33, EV(8)=12.63, EV(10)=17.50, EV(12)=22.83, EV(16)=35.19, EV(20)=49.50. Note sides alone give only ~50x across the whole game — sides are the MILESTONE, pips/speed/dice/shards are the MAGNITUDE. Keep it that way or side purchases stop feeling special.

MULTI-DICE
With D active dice, roll one index per die: k_1..k_D.
raw = SUM_d value_d(k_d)
combo(k_1..k_D):
  D == 1                                  -> 1.0
  all k_d equal                           -> 2 + D          (4x at D=2, 5x at D=3)
  sorted k_d are strictly consecutive      -> 1 + 0.6*D      (2.2x at D=2, 2.8x at D=3)
  otherwise                                -> 1.0
payout = raw * combo * G
Sanity check, D=2 with two d12s: P(all equal)=1/12=.0833, P(run)=2*11/144=.1528, E[combo]=.0833*4 + .1528*2.2 + .7639*1 = 1.433. Second die is therefore worth ~2.87x total income, which is what justifies its 400,000 price.

GLOBAL MULTIPLIER
G = (1 + 0.05*shards) * (1 + 0.02*tokens) * (1 + 0.05*achievements) * adBuff
  shards: permanent, from Recast. tokens: from 7-day streaks, cap 20 (max +40%). achievements: 12 max (max +60%). adBuff: 1.0 normally, 3.0 while a Lucky Streak is active.
All sources except adBuff are additive within their own term and multiplicative between terms — this stays legible and never explodes.

ROLL RATE
Manual: capped at ~2.2 taps/sec by the 380ms animation; taps queue rather than drop.
Auto: rollsPerSec(L) = 0.5 * 1.16^L for L >= 0 (L=0 granted at unlock). At L=25, rate = 20.0/sec, the hard cap. For L > 25: rate stays 20 and G gains an extra factor of 1.16^(L-25).
Total income/sec = (autoRate + manualRate) * E[payout].

OFFLINE (closed form, never simulated)
E_payout_cached is recomputed by a 4,000-sample Monte Carlo whenever sides/pips/dice change (~1ms).
offline = 0.5 * autoRate * E_payout_cached * min(elapsedSec, 14400)
Cap 14400s (4h); 'Night Die' upgrade extends to 28800s (8h); the welcome-back rewarded ad multiplies the 0.5 rate factor to 1.0 AND extends that session's cap to 28800s. Below 60 seconds elapsed, grant nothing and show nothing.

PRESTIGE (Recast)
Unlock at runCoins >= 2,000,000 (lifetime coins earned in the current run).
shardsGained = floor( 8 * (runCoins / 2e6)^0.45 )
  runCoins 2e6 -> 8 shards; 1e7 -> 16; 2e7 -> 22; 1e8 -> 45; 2e8 -> 63; 1e9 -> 130.
The curve's 0.45 exponent means waiting 10x longer gives only 2.8x the shards — a deliberate anti-hoarding shape that makes 'recast now' almost always correct, which is what keeps the cadence tight.
On Recast: all dice -> 1 side, all pips -> 0, coins -> 0, autoLevel -> floor(L/2).
Retained: shards, tokens, achievements, die slots owned, Night Die, and one Heirloom Face (chosen at the Recast screen) which returns at its pre-Recast pip level the moment the die reaches that side count again.

**Cost curves:** SIDES (the Nth side, N >= 2)
costSide(N) = round(25 * 2.9^(N-2))
N:  2=25, 3=73, 4=210, 5=610, 6=1769, 7=5130, 8=14878, 9=43146, 10=125124, 11=362861, 12=1.05M, 13=3.05M, 14=8.85M, 15=25.7M, 16=74.4M, 17=216M, 18=626M, 19=1.82B, 20=5.26B.
Growth rate 2.9. Chosen against combined income growth (sides ~N^1.6, plus pips, plus 1.16^L rate) so that early sides land seconds apart and late sides land 10-25 minutes apart. If a sim shows side 12-16 stalling past 30 minutes, drop 2.9 to 2.8 — do not touch base(k).

PIPS (level p on face k, p starting at 0)
costPip(k, p) = round(15 * base(k) * 1.26^p)
Face 1 level 0 = 15; face 6 level 0 = 270; face 6 level 10 = 2,712; face 12 level 20 = 82,000; face 20 level 30 = 1.62M.
Gain per level is 1.18x that face's value against a 1.26x cost — the 1.068 ratio makes pips a decelerating sink that can absorb any amount of coins without ever becoming the optimal-only purchase.
SHARPEN ALL cost = SUM over all faces of costPip(k, pip[k]); unlocks at 8 sides.

AUTO-ROLLER
costAuto(L) = round(400 * 1.62^L)
L: 0=400 (the unlock, 'BIND THE DIE'), 1=648, 2=1050, 5=4458, 10=49,700, 15=554k, 20=6.17M, 25=68.8M, 30=767M.
Growth 1.62 against a 1.16 rate gain — deliberately the worst cost-per-gain of the three sinks, so speed is the thing you buy when nothing else is affordable, and sides always feel like the priority.

DIE SLOTS (permanent, survive Recast)
costDie(d) = 400,000 * 90^(d-2)
d=2: 400,000 (requires die 1 at >= 10 sides). d=3: 36,000,000 (requires two dice at >= 12 sides each). d=4 would be 3.24B — out of scope for the prototype; the UI shows a locked fourth slot marked 'SOON' and nothing else.

NIGHT DIE (offline cap 4h -> 8h)
One-time, 2,000,000 coins, offered the first time the player returns after being away more than 3 hours.

BALANCE INVARIANTS A SIMULATOR SHOULD ASSERT
1. Time to first side purchase at 2.2 taps/sec, zero auto: 25 coins / 2.2 per sec = 11.4s. Must stay under 20s.
2. A greedy always-buy-cheapest-affordable bot must reach 6 sides in 5-8 minutes and the auto-roller in 1.5-3.5 minutes.
3. No single purchase may ever be more than 45 minutes of income away at its unlock point, except costSide(20) and costDie(3), which are the intended end-of-content walls.
4. Recast #2 must complete in 30-40 minutes; if it is under 25, lower the shard exponent from 0.45 to 0.40.
5. Income must never go DOWN after any purchase, including a Recast taken at the unlock threshold.

**Milestones:** First side bought (d1 -> d2) 0.2 min, Six sides (d6) 6 min, Auto-roller bound (hands-off from here on) 2.5 min, Second die slot purchased 55 min, First Recast 90 min, End of prototype content (3 dice at d20, ~5 Recasts) 420 min

**Die visual:** RENDERER: one HTML5 canvas, device-pixel-ratio scaled, ~300x300 CSS px centered in the viewport, redrawn only when something is animating (requestAnimationFrame is cancelled when idle and when document.hidden — this is the battery fix).

THE SHAPE, AS A FUNCTION OF SIDE COUNT N
The die is drawn as a flat "facet rosette": a regular N-gon outline plus N spokes from the centre to each vertex, cutting the interior into N triangular facets. Facet i carries the number i in a heavy geometric font, sized to fit the wedge. This reads simultaneously as a die (numbered faces, hard edges, a landed face) and as a shape that can gain one more of something, which a real polyhedron cannot do in 2D.

Vertex generation: p_i = (cx + R*cos(theta_i), cy + R*sin(theta_i)) with theta_i = -PI/2 + 2*PI*i/N.
Special-cased low N, because the first three shapes are the entire store listing:
- N=1: not a polygon. A pale stone circle with a single centred pip and a soft inner shadow. Deliberately inert-looking — the point is that it is boring.
- N=2: a lens/coin — two circular arcs meeting at the left and right vertices, bulge 0.35R. Facet 1 is the top half, facet 2 the bottom.
- N=3: equilateral triangle with rounded 6px corners.
- N=4: square rotated 45 degrees (diamond) with one extra interior line, giving the read of a tetrahedron's silhouette.
- N=5..: regular N-gon, corner rounding = 6px * (3/N) so high-N shapes approach a clean circle without the corners turning to mush.
- N >= 14: the interior number labels shrink past legibility, so facets stop being numbered and instead show 1..N as small pip clusters along the rim, with the currently-landed facet showing its full number in a large centred readout. Legibility beats completeness.
Radius scales gently with N: R = 96 + 2.2*min(N,20) px, so the die visibly gets BIGGER as it grows, capped at ~140px so it never touches the UI.

ADDING A SIDE: THE MORPH (the single most important animation in the game)
Do not pop from one polygon to the next. Sample both the N-gon and the (N+1)-gon at 240 equally spaced points along their perimeters (arc-length parameterised), then lerp point-for-point over 600ms with an easeOutBack (overshoot 1.7). The shape physically swells, rotates by half a facet, and settles. Simultaneously:
- the new facet's wedge draws in with a gold radial wipe from centre to rim over 300ms
- the new number stamps in at 1.6x scale and settles to 1.0
- a single expanding ring (stroke-only circle, radius 0 -> 2.2R, opacity 1 -> 0) over 500ms
- one short low thud + a rising chime
- the MAX ROLL readout under the die counts up digit by digit to the new base(N+1)
- all other UI freezes for 700ms, nothing else animates, no numbers tick. The die is the only thing moving.

ROLLING: THE TUMBLE (380ms)
- rotation: from current angle through 2 full turns plus the delta needed to bring the winning facet to 12 o'clock under a small fixed notch/pointer at the top. easeOutQuart.
- scale: keyframed 1.00 -> 0.88 (squash on the way down, 90ms) -> 1.06 (bounce, 190ms) -> 1.00 (380ms).
- a soft elliptical drop shadow under the die that widens and lightens as the die "lifts", then tightens on landing.
- On landing: the winning facet fills with the accent colour and flashes to white over 120ms, its number scales to 1.3x, and a coin chip reading "+1,247" spawns at the facet and arcs up to the coin counter over 450ms, which does a 120ms scale pop on arrival.
- A combo (matched set / run) overrides the landing: all matching facets flash together, the shape pulses twice, and the chip reads "+1,247 x4 MATCHED SET" in gold.

HIGH-SPEED MODE (auto-roll above 6 rolls/sec)
The tumble is skipped entirely. The die holds still with a faint continuous spin (0.4 rad/sec), the winning facet flashes for 80ms, and instead of one chip per roll, the coin counter rises smoothly (lerp toward true value) while small numerals spray from the rim at a throttled 8 per second regardless of actual roll rate. Above 14 rolls/sec the flashes merge into a soft pulsing glow. This caps the per-frame work at a constant no matter how fast the game gets, which is what keeps it at 60fps and off the battery-drain complaint list.

MULTIPLE DICE
Dice are laid out on one horizontal row, each shrinking to R*0.72 at D=2 and R*0.58 at D=3, all tumbling on the same tick so combos land visually simultaneously. Each die keeps its own side count and its own shape — seeing a d14 next to a freshly-recast d2 is the clearest possible picture of what Recast just did.

PALETTE AND POLICY
Geometric, clean, adult: charcoal background, bone-white die body, one warm accent (amber) for the landed facet, gold for combos. No faces, no eyes, no mascot, no nursery colours, no rounded cartoon styling. This is a deliberate policy choice, not just a style one — Google assesses the declared 13+ target audience against the actual imagery, and childlike art would drag the app into Families scope, which bans simulated gambling outright and would foreclose the slot reskin entirely.

**Slot reskin notes:** THE SEAM: a reel is a die with symbol faces. Write the prototype so the simulation layer never contains the string "dice", "side", or "face" in any user-visible position.

WHAT IS SHARED (100% of the engine)
- The die object {sides:N, faces:[{index, base, pip, label}], autoLevel} is already generic. A reel is the same object where faces[k].label is a symbol id instead of a number.
- "Buy a side" becomes "Add a symbol to the reel" with zero change to costSide, base(k), or the purchase flow. The reel starts with ONE symbol — which means the first thirty seconds are identical in shape to the dice game and carry the identical hook: a machine that starts with one symbol and earns more, one at a time.
- combo() is already a pattern evaluator over an array of rolled indices. The slot build replaces the two-rule dice version with a small payline table: three-of-a-kind -> value * 5, two-of-a-kind adjacent -> value * 2, full run -> value * 3.5. Same signature, same call site.
- Pips become "polish this symbol". Auto-roller becomes "autospin". Recast becomes "Refurbish the machine". Offline, dailies, ads, save format, and every formula and constant carry over unchanged.

WHAT CHANGES (the THEME object, one file section)
THEME = { renderShape(ctx, die, t), faceLabel(k), currencyName, palette, sfx, strings, comboTable }.
Dice theme: renderShape draws the facet rosette described above; faceLabel(k) returns the number k.
Slot theme: renderShape draws D vertical reel strips side by side; a spin scrolls each strip downward with motion blur and stops them left to right at 0ms/180ms/360ms with an easeOutBack overshoot on each; winning paylines draw a bright horizontal rule across the window and the symbols pulse. The strip is generated from the same faces array, so a reel with 4 symbols genuinely only has 4 things on it — the visible symbol pool growing IS the progression, exactly as the side count is in the dice game.

SHIP IT AS A SEPARATE PLAY LISTING, NOT AN IN-APP MODE
This is the load-bearing decision. Keeping the two as one app would apply the slot build's rating and ad classification to the dice build.
- The slot version meets Google's own definition of a social casino game (simulated gambling-style, nothing of real value to win). Answer the IARC simulated-gambling question YES — honestly. Misdeclaring is a suspension ground; a Teen/12+ rating is merely inconvenient.
- Expect ESRB "Simulated Gambling" and probably "Gambling Themes" from the visuals alone, i.e. Teen / PEGI 12. This forecloses Designed for Families, which does not matter because the dice build should not opt in either.
- AdMob may classify the inventory as gambling-related, which measurably reduces fill and eCPM and in the worst case produces no fill at all. This is the real business risk, and the cheap way to measure it is to ship dice first, record actual rewarded eCPM for a few weeks, then ship slot and compare the same metric before investing another hour in it.
- Use abstract original symbols (geometric glyphs, not fruit/7s/bells), no real casino brand names, logos, or trade dress. No cash out, no sweepstakes, no tournaments with prizes, no gift-card redemption — any of those moves the app from social-casino into full gambling rules.
- Include an in-app and store-listing disclaimer: "No real-money gambling. No prizes of real-world value. Intended for users of legal gambling age." Cheap insurance, one string in THEME.strings.
- Do not run paid user acquisition for the slot build; it requires Google Ads social-casino certification and a country allowlist. Irrelevant given zero marketing, but worth knowing before anyone is tempted.

The honest summary: the slot version costs a Teen rating and an unknown ad-revenue haircut, and buys a genuinely vacant niche (the closest free idle slot was delisted in 2024 at 2.13 stars). It is worth building only because the engine is already paid for. Do not build it until the dice build has a month of real eCPM data.

**Retention hooks:**
- THE SHAPE IS THE PROGRESS BAR. Every competitor shows progress as a number on a button. Here the object in the middle of the screen is visibly different at minute 1, minute 10, and hour 3. This is the only retention mechanic that also works as a store screenshot, which matters enormously when the marketing budget is zero.
- AUTOMATION IS MONOTONIC AND PERMANENT. Once the auto-roller is bought at ~2.5 minutes it is never removed, fuelled, throttled, or gated behind tapping again. The single loudest complaint about the genre leader's sequel is that the idle layer disappears; this game is structurally incapable of that, and saying so plainly in the store description is free differentiation.
- OFFLINE EARNINGS AT 50% RATE, 4-HOUR CAP (8 with the Night Die). The cap is the return reason: past 4 hours you are leaving money on the table, which pulls players back 2-4 times a day without a single notification. The welcome-back card shows exactly what accrued and offers the doubler ad — the highest-converting ad placement in the game, and one the player is genuinely happy to see.
- DAILY DIE WITH A FORGIVING STREAK. One ceremonial free roll per day paying 20 minutes of income times a streak multiplier (1.0 -> 3.0 over seven days), with one automatic streak forgiveness per week. Day 7 mints a permanent +2% token. The forgiveness is the point: punishing a missed day is how you convert a lapsed player into a churned one.
- RECAST CADENCE OF 30-40 MINUTES AFTER THE FIRST. The 0.45 exponent on the shard formula deliberately makes waiting unprofitable, so the loop resolves inside a single sitting. Each Recast is a complete, satisfying arc — melt the die, watch it grow back four times faster, feel the shards — which is exactly the shape of a session a commuter or a lunch break can contain.
- RECAST GIVES BACK, NEVER JUST TAKES. Half your auto-roll levels survive, the die slots survive, and one Heirloom Face of your choosing comes back fully upgraded. The first Recast screen leads with what you KEEP, not what you lose, because the first prestige is where idle games bleed players.
- ONE DIE, ONE SCREEN, NO WIKI. Every mechanic is introduced one at a time, each with a single sentence, and nothing is ever hidden behind a submenu. What players praise in the leader's sequel is drip-fed legibility; what they hate is UI sprawl from parallel dice and a bolted-on deckbuilder. A single evolving object on a phone screen is structurally immune to both.
- EVERY PURCHASE HAS AN IMMEDIATE, VISIBLE PAYOFF. The MAX ROLL readout ticks up on every side. The new facet appears in the very next roll. The obscure-dice-incremental failure mode is players not understanding what the central die does; here the answer is always on screen in one line.
- THE SECOND DIE IS AN EVENT, NOT A MENU ITEM. It arrives around 55 minutes, only after the first die is a fully-realised d10+, and it arrives at d1 — so the player immediately gets to re-experience the game's best five minutes with all their multipliers intact.
- TWELVE ACHIEVEMENTS, EACH +5%, EACH NAMING A MOMENT. 'Six Sides', 'Bound the Die', 'Matched Set', 'First Recast', 'Twenty Faces'. They cost almost nothing to implement and give the pause screen a reason to exist.

**Monetization hooks:**
- LUCKY STREAK (the workhorse): rewarded ad for x3 all income for 3 minutes. 7-minute cooldown, first offered around minute 4, never auto-plays, always dismissible with one tap. During the buff the die glows and the background shifts warm, so the player can see what they bought. Expect this to be 50-60% of total ad impressions.
- OFFLINE DOUBLER (the highest-intent placement): on the welcome-back card, a rewarded ad that raises the offline rate from 50% to 100% AND extends that session's cap from 4h to 8h. The player is already looking at a concrete number and is being offered exactly double it. One per return, no cooldown beyond that.
- CAST A FREE SIDE: once per Recast, when the player is within 60% of the next side's cost, a rewarded ad grants 40% of that side's cost in coins. Deterministic, disclosed, no randomness — which is the whole reason it never touches loot-box odds-disclosure rules. Framed as 'a hand up', not a gate.
- TIME SKIP: rewarded ad granting 30 minutes of current income, 3 per day, offered from the pause menu only (never pushed). Low volume, high value, and it self-balances because it scales with current income rather than a fixed amount.
- DAILY DIE REROLL: one rewarded ad to reroll a disappointing Daily Die result. Tiny revenue, but it converts the one moment per day where the player feels unlucky into a moment where they feel helped.
- INTERSTITIALS: ZERO for the first three sessions and the first 60 minutes of play, full stop. After that, at most one per 20 minutes, only on the Recast confirmation screen (a natural pause where the player has already committed), skippable after 5 seconds, never during a roll, never between a purchase and its animation, never in front of a reward.
- IDEMPOTENT LOCAL GRANT (the most important line in the ad code): write a pending-reward record to save state BEFORE calling the SDK, call with a 12-second timeout, and grant the reward on success, failure, timeout, no-fill, or app-resume-with-pending-record alike. The genre leader's iOS rating sits a full point below its Android rating specifically because an ads update froze the game and withheld promised bonuses. An ad that fails must cost the player nothing and must never be noticed.
- NO IAP AT LAUNCH, AND SAY SO. The leader's top SKU is $49.99. Shipping with zero purchases, ads-optional, and a store description that states it plainly is the single cheapest piece of positioning available to a developer with no marketing budget, and it is exactly the pitch that lands on r/incremental_games.
- PHASE TWO ONLY, IF THE NUMBERS JUSTIFY IT: a single one-time 'Remove Interstitials + keep all rewarded ads' purchase around $2.99. Never a currency pack, never a randomised anything, never a paywalled side. Coins must remain non-purchasable and non-redeemable forever — that one rule is what keeps the app entirely outside Play's Real-Money Gambling policy.

**Risks:**
- ECONOMY TUNING IS THE WHOLE GAME AND THE CONSTANTS HERE ARE UNVERIFIED. The 2.9 side growth rate, 1.62 auto cost rate, and 0.45 shard exponent are educated starting points, not measured ones. Before any polish, write a 40-line headless simulator that runs a greedy buy-cheapest-affordable bot for 8 hours of game time and prints time-to-each-milestone. Expect to change these numbers three or four times. This is a one-evening job and skipping it is the most likely way the project fails.
- SIDES ALONE ONLY PROVIDE ~50x INCOME ACROSS THE ENTIRE GAME (EV(1)=1 to EV(20)=49.5), while side costs grow 2.9x each. If pips, auto-rate, dice, and shards are not carrying the rest, the game hard-stalls somewhere around side 11-13. Assert invariant #3 in the simulator.
- THE HOOK MAY NOT SURVIVE PAST d8. 'Watch the shape grow' is genuinely novel and genuinely thin — by side 12 the visual delta between a 12-gon and a 13-gon is nearly nothing. The prototype must prove that the per-face pip layer and the second die carry the mid-game, or the game is a great five minutes attached to a mediocre three hours. Test this on a real phone before building anything else.
- REWARDED-AD REVENUE AT THIS SCALE IS SMALL. The closest comparable with varied side counts got ~5,600 downloads; the category leader took years to reach ~500k. At realistic rewarded eCPM, a few hundred dollars a month needs tens of thousands of engaged installs. The plan is sound but the expected value should be stated honestly to the owner up front.
- CAPACITOR + CANVAS PERFORMANCE ON LOW-END ANDROID. A 60fps canvas loop on a $120 phone is not free. The high-speed render mode and the 'cancel rAF when hidden' rule are mandatory, not optional. Test on the cheapest Android device available, not on the developer's phone.
- AD SDK FAILURE MODES ARE THE MOST LIKELY SOURCE OF ONE-STAR REVIEWS. No fill, timeouts, and frozen post-ad states are normal, not exceptional. The idempotent local grant must be written first and tested with the SDK deliberately disabled.
- SAVE CORRUPTION AND CLOCK ABUSE. localStorage in a Capacitor webview can be cleared by the OS; write to Capacitor Preferences with a localStorage mirror, version the save schema from v1, and clamp offline time to [0, cap] so a system-clock change can neither grant infinite coins nor produce a negative.
- POLICY DRIFT INTO FAMILIES SCOPE. A cute die is a policy liability, not just a style choice. Google assesses the declared target audience against the actual art and listing wording, and a mismatch is a suspension ground. Declare 13+, keep the art geometric, never opt into Designed for Families, and never use 'for kids' language anywhere.
- THE SLOT RESKIN MAY NOT PAY FOR ITSELF. It buys a Teen rating and a possible AdMob restricted-inventory classification that reduces or eliminates fill, in exchange for a vacant niche. Do not build it until the dice build has a month of real eCPM data to compare against.
- SCOPE CREEP IS THE DEFAULT FAILURE MODE FOR A SOLO HOBBYIST. The genre's cautionary tale is a sequel that added a deckbuilder and got its worst reviews for UI bloat. Every item in out_of_scope is there because adding it would make this worse, not better.
- A 1,500-2,500 LINE SINGLE FILE IS TIGHT FOR THIS. Rough budget: engine and formulas 350, save/offline 150, ad layer 150, canvas renderer and animations 500, UI and layout 450, theme object and strings 150, dailies/achievements/Recast screens 300. Anything not on that list does not fit.

**Out of scope:**
- Per-face EDITING (swapping, combining, re-rolling what is on a face). This is the correct phase-two content patch and the faces-as-objects data model exists specifically to make it cheap later — but it is a second full system with its own UI and it does not fit in the prototype.
- Any second currency. One currency, plus Shards and Tokens which are permanent counters with no spending decisions attached.
- Cards, decks, duels, PvP, towers, roulette, minigames, or any system that is not the die. This is the specific mistake the genre leader's sequel made.

**Ideas worth grafting:**
- THE BACKBONE: design 0's exponential face-value ladder V(n)=ceil(1.55^(n-1)) paired with side cost 1.95^(n-2). This is the only formula pair in the panel where the die's expected value grows exponentially with side count, so '+ SIDE' remains the correct purchase from minute 0 to the end of content and time-per-side lengthens by a controlled ~1.30x. Adopt this wholesale and throw out both k^1.6 (design 2) and flat +0.5 (design 1).
- Design 0's rule that the first auto-roll level must be strictly faster than a comfortable thumb (2.0 rolls/s vs ~1.7/s measured). Make it a hard acceptance test on a real phone, not a constant. Both other designs ship automation at a quarter of tap speed.
- Design 2's prestige retention package: die slots are permanent, auto-roll keeps floor(L/2), and one Heirloom Face returns at full pip level once the die is big enough again. It is the only prestige that leads with what you keep, and it removes the 'back to mashing a one-sided ball' hole in design 0.
- Design 2's anti-hoarding prestige exponent (shards ~ runCoins^0.45, so 10x the wait yields only 2.8x shards) so recasting promptly is always correct and a run fits in one sitting. Design 1's ^0.42 is the same idea.
- Design 2's explicit balance invariants plus a 40-line headless greedy-bot simulator as a gate before any art or polish. Add design 0's extra invariant: combo/variance income must never exceed ~40% of measured income. Make 'income must never decrease after any purchase, including a Recast taken at the unlock threshold' a hard assert.
- Design 0's sink-retirement rule: a sink auto-hides when its payback time exceeds 3x the headline purchase's payback time. Generalise it - any button that is dominated for more than a minute disappears rather than sitting there as a trap.
- Design 1's closed forms and caps for effect faces, IF effect faces are ever built in phase two: Double steady state (1-q)/(1-2q), Reroll 1/(1-r), caps at 0.40n on each, iterative resolution with a hard chain cap, and closed-form collapse at high auto rates. The maths is right and the caps are load-bearing.
- One currency, non-purchasable, non-redeemable, every price fixed and disclosed, no randomised purchase anywhere (all three designs, unanimously). Keeps the whole thing out of gambling and loot-box policy and costs nothing.
- Closed-form offline earnings at 50% rate with a hard cap as the return reason, plus design 0's SHORTFALL ad placement (offered only when the player is within ~20% of affording the next side) - it targets the exact minute the exponential curve would otherwise close the app.
- Design 2's forgiving daily streak (one automatic forgiveness per 7 days). Cheap, and not punishing a lapse is how you keep the players you already have.
- Design 0's + DIE born at one side while side-buying auto-targets the smallest die, so a new die replays the best two minutes of the game with late-game income behind it. Free content from an existing button.

**Ideas rejected:**
- Design 1's flat +0.5 EV per side against a 1.95^n cost, and its instruction 'sides stall naturally around n=14-20 - do not fix this, it is the pacing'. This makes the owner's core purchase strictly dominated by the grind sink by minute ten. Reject outright.
- Design 2's 2.9x side cost growth against k^1.6 income. Side 11 is ~139,000 coins per EV point while a pip is ~830. Same failure, steeper.
- Design 2's endgame milestone table. Side 20 at 5.26 billion coins is roughly 42 hours of income at the income level that reaches it; '3 dice at d20 and 5 Recasts in 420 minutes' is off by an order of magnitude and should not be quoted to anyone.
- Any auto-roller that unlocks below human tap rate: design 1's 0.60 rolls/s against a 2.5/s manual cap, and design 2's 0.5 rolls/s against 2.2/s. Paying coins to slow yourself down is the worst possible first automation beat.
- Design 0's RECAST wiping the ROLLER entirely, and design 1's Recast dropping autoLevel to 1 while claiming automation is never taken away. Both contradict their own headline retention promise. Automation must survive prestige at least at half strength.
- Design 0's combo constants: c_hat of 1.35 / 1.8 / 2.4 / 3.2 for 2-5 dice. Real values on same-ladder dice are roughly 1.05 to 1.6. The whole multi-dice income rate is overstated ~2x. Also drop the m=5 -> x50 tier; it makes several minutes of income land in one animation.
- Design 1's WILD face (pays the value of the die's highest face). It is a strictly dominant carve priced by a formula unrelated to its gain, so it becomes a mandatory auto-buy and erases the build decision it was meant to create.
- Design 2's three stacked additive multiplier sources (shards +5%, tokens +2% capped at 20, achievements +5% x12). Three save fields, three UI surfaces, at most +100% combined against costs growing 2.9x per side. Collapse to one.
- Design 2's 4,000-sample Monte Carlo for E[payout]. The expectation is a one-line closed-form sum over faces; the MC only adds variance to welcome-back numbers.
- An additive prestige multiplier as the ONLY long-run scaling, which all three designs share (M = 1 + c * shards) against exponential cost curves. Design 0's version literally converges to a fixed point after ~3-4 runs. Either make shards compound, or have prestige reduce cost growth, or accept and state plainly that content ends at run 3.
- Design 0's 'there is never a choice to make' taken to its conclusion - auto-targeted sides plus a self-hiding FILE plus unlock-gated everything else leaves an economy with literally zero decisions. Keep exactly one live tradeoff (one cheap sink competing with the next side) and no more.
- Design 2's Night Die, tokens, 12 achievements and ceremonial Daily Die in the prototype. None of them are the die growing a side; all of them are economy surface to tune and save-migrate. Cut to the offline card plus one daily free side.
